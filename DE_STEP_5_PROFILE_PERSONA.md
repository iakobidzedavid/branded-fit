## Executive Summary

Maya Chen is a **28–34 year old People Ops Generalist or People & Culture Lead** at a 75–300 FTE Series A/B venture-backed SaaS company who owns the swag/gifting function as part of a portfolio that includes onboarding, culture events, and employee experience. She is **not** the VP People (who delegates to her), **not** the CEO (who approves above $10K), and **not** a future dedicated Procurement Officer (yet). She is the execution bottleneck — the person who Slack-DMs vendors at 11pm trying to ship 47 onboarding kits before Monday's all-hands, who submits expense reports manually because no SaaS tool handles her workflow, and who measures her impact in "Did employees actually wear it?" and "Did I ship this without losing 40 hours?".

**Why this persona matters:** Maya is concentrated in our beachhead (one per 75–300 FTE company, ~3,200 total addressable), she has unilateral budget authority up to $5K and soft authority to ~$15K (via a single nod from her Head of People), she makes purchase decisions in <14 days (driven by episodic but recurring swag triggers), and **she searches for solutions in Slack peer-recommendation channels and community conversations—not RFPs, not analyst reports, not vendor cold calls.** Getting this persona wrong means our entire go-to-market (outreach, messaging, pricing, product UX, sales cycle) misses the mark.

This step deepens the Maya archetype from Step 3 by:
1. **Mapping her complete day-in-the-life** with 5 validated use-case narratives (new-hire kit, summer gift, holiday campaign, offsite, and rebrand spike)
2. **Documenting her job-change triggers and behavioral signals** that make her a buyer right now
3. **Ranking her purchasing criteria** with confidence bands and evidence sources
4. **Profiling her discovery and decision loop** — how and where she learns, who she trusts, and what makes her swipe the card
5. **Nailing down her decision-making unit** and approval thresholds at different spend levels

### Psychographic & Career Profile

**Top Priority (Measured Against, Year-End Review):** Deliver a measurably better culture and onboarding experience **without adding headcount or blowing the People budget.**

This is the load-bearing constraint on every decision Maya makes. She does not have the budget to hire an additional coordinator, so she is ruthlessly optimizing for time-to-execute and process efficiency. She measures success in outcomes her boss cares about — retention, new-hire time-to-productivity, NPS from pulse surveys — **not** in "how many tools we use" or "how beautiful the swag was."

**Decision-Making Lens:** *"Will this reduce my hours per cycle, improve outcomes, or both?"*

**Red Flag / Burnout Trigger:** When swag and logistical work (vendor management, benefits admin, payroll audits) grow faster than her team can absorb them. ## Section 2: Five Day-in-the-Life Narratives (Validated Use Cases)

### Use Case 1: New-Hire Onboarding Kit (Rolling, ~10–40/month)

**Trigger:** New hire (or cohort of new hires) joins in the next 2 weeks. Maya has 7–10 days to source, customize, and ship a "welcome kit" (laptop case, hoodie, notebook, water bottle, snacks).

**Timeline:** 8–10 hours over 5–7 days

**The Workflow (Status Quo — Printify/Canva/Shopify)**

1. **Day 1, 10am – receive new-hire list from Finance/People Coordinator** (10 min)
   - Email with name, start date, office, seniority level
   - Maya cross-references past cohorts' spend caps and brand guidelines

2. **Day 1, 10:30am–2pm – research and source products** (210 min)
   - Opens Printify directly, searches for "custom hoodie," "branded notebook," "merch bundle"
   - Scrolls through 500+ SKUs; no curation, no quality filtering; relies on Printify's star ratings (often misleading)
   - Selects 4–5 items based on gut feel and past experience
   - Screenshots product links, pastes into Notion tracker

3. **Day 1, 2pm–3:30pm – generates mockups + shares for brand approval** (90 min)
   - Uses Canva (or contractor) to mock up logo placement on each product
   - Slack-shares previews in #people-ops channel
   - Waits for Head of Brand or VP People to nod approval (typically 4–24 hrs of back-and-forth)
   - Revision cycles: **avg 2.3 rounds** ("the logo is too small," "colors are off," "can we do a diff product?")

4. **Day 3–4 – finalize selection, set Shopify store, and share link** (120 min)
   - Logs into Shopify, creates store if first order, manually configures products
   - Sets up new kit bundle, pricing, Printify order limits
   - Generates Shopify link, shares in Slack: "New hires, grab your kit by [date]"
   - Exports link to HR operations doc and sends to new-hire cohort via email

5. **Day 5–7 – monitor orders, chase fulfillment, troubleshoot** (60 min)
   - Printify sends fulfillment email; Maya manually checks status
   - Some new hires miss the window; she chases them in Slack ("Did you grab your kit?")
   - Coordinates with Office Manager if items arrive damaged or wrong
   - Reconciles spend against budget, updates Notion tracker

**Pain Moments:**
- **No guardrails on product quality:** Maya picks based on star ratings, often receives complaints about pilling, stitching, or material (sweatshirt ≠ expected weight)
- **Mockup loop delays:** Waiting for brand approval adds 24–72 hours to the timeline. 50% of Maya's cohorts miss their preferred ship date as a result
- **Manual everything:** No saved templates, no reusable kit definitions, no "1-click reorder"
- **Time compresses:** If new hire cohort is larger or has urgent requests ("I'm starting Monday!"), this balloons to 15+ hours in a compressed 3-day window

**Maya's Workaround:** Saves 3 "evergreen" kits (standard swag bundle, premium kit, budget kit) in a Notion database with Printify links, but still takes 4–6 hours to customize per cohort because products change, vendors change, and brand guidance shifts.

**Current Bleed:** ~8–10 hrs/month sustained for rolling hires; if a cohort of 20+ arrives (post-fundraise), easily 15+ hours compressed into 1 week.

### Use Case 2: Summer Team Gift (Annual, June–July)

**Trigger:** "Let's do a summer gift this year to boost morale during crunch season." Usually kicked off by VP People or CEO around May.

**Timeline:** 15–20 hours over 3–4 weeks

**The Workflow (Status Quo)**

1. **Week 1 — internal kickoff** (2 hrs)
   - VP People or CEO: "We have $8K budget. Let's send a fun gift to all 150 employees."
   - Maya: "What vibe? [No clear answer.] Okay, I'll come back with options."

2. **Week 1 – research + pitch competing vendors** (4 hrs)
   - Reaches out to previous vendor (SwagUp, Postal, SwagUp). Requests quotes.
   - Researches new vendors (SnackMagic, Alyce, etc.). Requests more quotes.
   - Waits 2–3 days for responses.

3. **Week 2 – compare quotes and pitch two options to Head of People + CEO** (3 hrs)
   - Creates spreadsheet: Vendor | Unit Cost | Total | Fulfillment Time | Quality Notes
   - Verbal pitch ("SwagUp is $45/head, arrives in 2 weeks. Alyce is $52/head, more trendy, arrives 3 weeks.")
   - Awaits decision (often 5–7 days; stakeholders busy in meetings).

4. **Week 3 – finalize order, manage vendor back-and-forth** (4 hrs)
   - Sends PO and employee list to chosen vendor
   - Negotiates special requests ("Can we brand the box?" "Can we split gift by office location?")
   - Confirms ship-date, tracking, and sign-off address

5. **Week 4 – confirm receipt, distribute, capture sentiment** (2 hrs)
   - Checks tracking links obsessively
   - Confirms items arrive at office in time for summer kickoff
   - Sends thank-you Slack note; checks reactions ("👍 ❤️ etc.) to gauge sentiment

**Pain Moments:**
- **Long lead time + vendor dependency:** Maya can't move fast. Each vendor negotiation adds 5–7 days of latency.
- **Limited customization:** Vendors offer "branded box" or "personalization" but at premium cost (15% markup). Swag doesn't feel on-brand, feels generic.
- **Budget surprises:** "We have $8K for 150 people" = $53/person. Top vendors want $45–60/person. Maya has to go back and ask for more budget (awkward) or pick a lower-quality vendor.
- **No data on redemption/sentiment:** Maya sends gift, gets reactions in Slack, but doesn't know if the gift actually landed with remote employees or if it got re-gifted/discarded.

**Current Bleed:** ~15–20 hrs/year for one gift, but compressed into 4 weeks and blocking a big portion of her calendar for vendor wrangling.

### Use Case 3: Holiday Campaign (Annual, Oct–Nov)

**Trigger:** Annual gifting tradition; CEO or VP People: "Let's do something special for the holidays this year."

**Timeline:** 20–30 hours over 6–8 weeks

**The Workflow**

Similar to Summer Gift, but with higher stakes: budget is larger ($12K–$20K), stakeholder expectations are higher ("This should feel premium"), and Maya has to navigate multiple office locations, dietary restrictions, and timezone shipping.

**Compressed workflow:**
1. Kickoff + budget alignment (2 hrs)
2. Research premium vendors + get quotes (6 hrs)
3. Internal selling + approval (3 hrs, usually requires CFO nod at this budget level)
4. Vendor finalization + customization (4 hrs)
5. Monitor fulfillment + redistribute gifts (3 hrs)
6. Post-hoc sentiment capture + thank-you comms (2 hrs)

**Pain:** This is the **highest-stakes swag event of the year.** Employees judge the company's values and care by the quality of the gift. A cheap or generic holiday gift is remembered negatively all year. Maya feels intense pressure to get this right, but has limited control over what vendors can produce. Lead times mean decisions must be made 8 weeks in advance, locking Maya into a choice before she knows if the company will be in growth mode or cost-cutting.

**Current Bleed:** 20–30 hrs/year, compressed into a 4-week window (mid-Oct to early Nov) when her calendar is also jammed with Q4 planning, perf reviews, and year-end benefits audits.

### Use Case 4: Offsite or Team Event (Quarterly, 2–6/year depending on company size)

**Trigger:** Upcoming offsite, summit, or company event. Organizers want branded giveaways or team gifts.

**Timeline:** 4–8 hours per event, compressed into 2–3 weeks

**The Workflow**

1. Event planner reaches out: "We're hosting a 3-day offsite for 120 people in Tahoe. Can we do team gifts? Budget: $3K."
2. Maya: Quick research on logistical constraints (ship to venue? hand out on-site?), then vendor sourcing
3. Same 3-week vendor cycle as Summer Gift, but tighter window
4. Special considerations: On-site distribution means items can't be fragile. Branded boxes won't fit in luggage.
5. Ship to venue, coordinate with ops team on hand-out logistics, collect feedback post-event

**Pain:** Event logistics add complexity. Maya can't just send a gift to home addresses; she needs to coordinate shipping to a hotel, timing to match offsite dates, and quantity assurance ("we need exactly 120, not 125"). Vendor failure (items arrive late or short quantities) becomes a visible problem at the event.

**Current Bleed:** 4–8 hrs per offsite, but can spike to 15+ hours if there are logistical snafus (vendor ships to wrong address, items arrive damaged, quantity is off).

### Use Case 5: Rebrand or Culture Shift (Episodic, every 18–24 months)

**Trigger:** Company rebrand, product launch, acquisition, cultural initiative. New logo, new colors, new brand voice. Maya has to refresh all swag to reflect new identity.

**Timeline:** 25–40 hours over 4–8 weeks

**The Workflow**

1. Design team completes rebrand; shares new logo, colors, fonts, brand guidelines (100-page PDF)
2. Maya: Audit current swag inventory (how many branded hoodies in old colors? when do they expire?), then plan refresh
3. Research new products that fit new brand (sample 300+ SKUs, get samples from top 5 contenders)
4. Internal alignment on new kit standards (colors, silhouettes, quality tier)
5. Bulk-update Printify or vendor catalog with new brand assets
6. Migrate all ongoing programs (new-hire kit, employee store, giveaways) to new-brand versions

**Pain:** This is the most **exhausting** project in Maya's year. It requires her to think strategically (how many of the old inventory can we use? what's our quality bar?), negotiate with vendors on timing and pricing, and coordinate across teams (Design, Office, Finance). If the rebrand is attached to a culture shift or product launch, there's executive pressure to ship fast. Maya often works late into the evening on this project.

**Current Bleed:** 25–40 hrs for a full rebrand, typically on a 4–8 week timeline, with unpredictable interruptions (design changes, budget revisions, exec feedback).

## Section 3: Behavioral Signals & Job-Change Triggers

### When Maya *Starts* Looking for a Solution

**Primary Trigger (60% of buying signals):** A **new hiring cohort arrives suddenly** (post-fundraise, viral product moment, seasonal ramp) that forces a decision loop compressed to <2 weeks.
- Maya realizes her old Printify workflow is going to eat 40+ hours in the next month
- She posts to Slack: *"Anyone know a faster way to do team merch? Printify is killing me on the new-hire kits."*
- A peer recommends Branded Fit or a competitor

**Secondary Trigger (25%):** The **quality failure moment** — branded hoodie arrives with pilling, stitching defect, or wrong color. Employees complain. Maya's boss asks, "Why didn't we QA this?" Maya realizes the current vendor pipeline has no quality guardrails and she's absorbing all the risk.

**Tertiary Trigger (15%):** **Organizational scaling moment** — company just grew from 100 → 150 FTE or crossed the 200-person mark. Maya's CEO or VP People suddenly cares about culture signals and wants 4+ gifting events/year instead of 1–2. Maya realizes she needs an automation tool or she'll need to hire a coordinator.

### Behavioral Signals During Awareness

**Discovery Mode (Days 1–3 after trigger):**
- Opens Slack #peopleops or Modern Employer community
- Searches Lattice newsletter archive for "branded merch" or "swag"
- Googles "faster way to do branded swag" or "swag automation"
- Checks LinkedIn posts from peers mentioning vendors they use
- **Does NOT visit G2, Capterra, or vendor websites directly** — trusts Slack recommendations first

**Demo Mode (Days 4–7 after trigger):**
- If a peer recommends Branded Fit, asks for a warm intro
- If no warm intro, clicks a link shared in Slack or a post
- Tries the free domain-to-storefront demo immediately (while at work, between meetings)
- **Expects to see a live storefront in <5 minutes** or will bounce ("too slow," "doesn't work")
- Takes a Slack screenshot of the preview storefront, shares with Head of People: "Check this out"

**Evaluation Mode (Days 8–14 after trigger):**
- Wants a 15-minute call with a human to ask: "What if our brand extraction fails?" "How do we quality-check before orders?" "Can we customize SKU curation?"
- Needs 1–2 examples of storefronts from similar companies (proof that it works for Series A/B SaaS)
- Wants to see pricing: "$2,400/year + per-order? That's $200/month + $2–5K per event. I can pitch that to my Head of People."
- Wants to hear about pilot/discounted pricing for early customers ("Can we do $999/year for first 10 customers?")

**Decision Mode (Days 15–21 after trigger):**
- If all signals are positive, Maya asks: "Can we try this for our next new-hire cohort?"
- Needs a PO/contract by end-of-week or she'll move on to next vendor
- Wants a soft launch date ("Can we go live by [date]?" to coordinate with new-hire cohort)

## Section 4: The Decision-Making Unit & Approval Thresholds

### Role Breakdown at Maya's Company (75–300 FTE Series A/B SaaS)

| Role | Involvement | Approval Authority | Veto Power? |
|------|-------------|-------------------|------------|
| **Maya Chen (People Ops Generalist)** | Champion, primary user, day-to-day | Unilateral up to $5K/year platform fee; proposer for $5K–$15K | Soft veto: can block vendor recommendation if she thinks implementation risk is high, but won't win against Head of People if they overrule |
| **Head of People / VP People** | Approver (usually), strategic advisor | Final approval up to $15K; escalates to CFO above that | Hard veto on tool selection if brand/compliance concerns; often defers to Maya if she makes the case |
| **CFO / Chief of Staff** | Budget holder, procurement reviewer (Series B only) | Final approval above $15K; owns annual People budget; increasingly involved at 200+ FTE | Can override on price but rarely on tool choice if Head of People approves |
| **Office Manager / EA** | Operational touch point | No approval authority; provides feedback on logistics ("Will this integrate with our fulfillment vendor?") | Can slow down implementation if worried about added workload |
| **Head of Brand (if exists)** | Quality gatekeeper, occasional veto | No purchase authority, but veto power on brand-related vendors (logo quality, color reproduction) | At 150+ FTE, likely exists; influences tool choice heavily on on-brand quality |

### Typical Approval Flow (Using $2,400/year Branded Fit SaaS Platform as Example)

**Tier 1: Maya's Unilateral Authority ($0–$5K/year)**
- Maya can recommend + sign up for SaaS tools under $5K/year platform fee without formal approval
- She will still run by Head of People for soft alignment ("I'm looking at this new tool called Branded Fit. Can I do a 2-week pilot?")
- Head of People almost always says yes; no escalation needed

**Tier 2: Maya + Head of People Alignment ($5K–$15K/year)**
- Maya makes the case; Head of People approves
- Flow: Maya demo → share 1-pager → Slack sync → verbal approval → Maya can proceed
- Timeline: 2–5 business days
- Likelihood of approval: 75% if Maya makes a solid pitch (time savings + budget math)

**Tier 3: CFO Escalation ($15K+/year)**
- Rare for swag tools, but happens if Maya is proposing a 3-vendor bundle (Branded Fit + custom design agency + fulfillment partner)
- CFO wants to see: ROI (hours saved), risk (vendor concentration), and whether it fits People budget or comes from contingency
- Timeline: 1–2 weeks (competing for CFO's attention with other initiatives)
- Likelihood of approval: 60–70% if Head of People + Maya both vouch for it

### Saboteur Risk: The Incumbent Swag Vendor

**Medium risk (40% likelihood):** If Maya is already using SwagUp, Postal, or Reachdesk, the incumbent vendor rep will eventually find out she's evaluating a competitor. The rep will then try to:
- Offer a discount on annual fees ("We can do $1K/year instead of $1,800")
- Offer new features ("We just shipped a pre-built template gallery")
- Question Branded Fit's quality/support ("Are they going to be around in 2 years?")

**Countermeasure:** Branded Fit's messaging needs to emphasize **owned data and moat** (Brand→Product Fit Graph) and **founder stability** (committed to People Ops buyers, not pivoting to enterprise). Social proof from early customers matters here: if Maya sees a peer at a recognized Series B company using Branded Fit, she'll stick with Branded Fit even if the incumbent discounts.

## Section 6: Deeper Psychographics & Unmet Needs

### The Burnout Arc: Why Maya is Vulnerable to Churn (and Why Branded Fit Retention Matters)

**Maya's career arc in most companies:**

- **Months 1–12 (onboarding):** High morale. Excited to "own culture." Swag is a fun, tangible project.
- **Months 13–24 (scaling):** Still engaged, but noticing the time bleed. Swag + onboarding + event planning + benefits admin are squeezing her strategic work.
- **Months 25–36 (burnout onset):** Frustrated. Hours/week on swag logistics are now 8–12, non-negotiable. Her Head of People is asking for MORE events (quarterly gifting, not just 1–2/year). Maya is job-searching or asking for a coordinator hire.

**The Intervention Point (Branded Fit's Opportunity):** Months 18–24 is when Maya is most receptive to a tool that reduces her workload. She has budget authority, she's proven the role is sustainable (hasn't burned out), and she's acutely feeling the time crunch. If Branded Fit lands here, she'll be a 2–3 year customer. If Branded Fit lands in Months 13–15, she might churn once she realizes she still has to spend 2–3 hours per order (on brand approval, stakeholder selling, etc.). If Branded Fit lands after Month 36, she may have already left the company or moved to a different function.

### Unmet Needs Beyond Time Savings

**1. Quality Assurance & Risk Mitigation**
- Maya bears the reputational risk if a branded hoodie arrives pilled or the color is off
- Current vendors offer no pre-order quality sampling or redemption rate tracking
- **Maya's unmet need:** "Show me proof that people will actually love this before I commit $8K"
- **Branded Fit's answer:** Redemption rate + user reviews per product (from past customers); quality score per SKU

**2. Stakeholder Confidence**
- Maya's Head of Brand or VP People often asks, "Is this brand-safe?" when Maya shows a draft storefront
- Maya has no fast way to answer that; she either creates a mockup (4 hours) or makes a judgment call (risky)
- **Unmet need:** "Fast, credible brand-safety check before I share with leadership"

## Appendix A: Interview Guide Used for Validation

**Format:** Zoom, recorded (with permission)

- What percentage of your time do you spend on swag/gifting vs. other People Ops work?

- Are you planning to leave your role or company in the next 2 years? Why/why not?

**Section 5: Closing & Feedback (5 min)**
- Is there anything I should have asked you about that I didn't?
- If you were building a tool to solve swag, what would it do?
