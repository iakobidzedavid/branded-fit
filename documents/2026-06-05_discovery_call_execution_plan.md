## Executive Summary

This document provides the end-to-end framework for executing discovery calls with Series B–D People Ops prospects who have responded positively to Branded Fit's warm outreach campaign. The calls will validate three critical assumptions from Step 20-21:

1. **Assumption #2 (VP People $25K signing authority)** — Testing whether People Ops leaders can approve $24K commitments
2. **Assumption #3 (Maya WTP $24K)** — Direct pricing feedback on the $24K Growth tier annual commitment
3. **Assumption #6 (warm-intro-to-pilot conversion 15%)** — Measuring willingness to move from discovery to pilot

Each call follows a structured 30–45 minute script, embeds validation questions in live demonstrations, and documents objections and feature requests for product prioritization. Calls are tracked in a dedicated discovery call notes spreadsheet that feeds into product roadmap and messaging refinements.

## Part 1: Pre-Call Preparation

### 1.1 Prospect Selection & Qualification Checklist

Before scheduling a call, verify that the prospect meets the Maya Chen persona:

**Confirm via warm-outreach response or LinkedIn:**
- [ ] Title contains: People Ops, HR Ops, Culture, People Manager, Talent Manager, or similar
- [ ] Company size: 200–1,500 FTE (verify via Crunchbase, LinkedIn, or direct ask)
- [ ] Company is remote-distributed OR has significant distributed workforce (not single HQ-based)
- [ ] Company has previously purchased or actively manages company swag/gifts
- [ ] Initial response shows genuine interest (not automated/generic reply)

**Red flags to exclude:**
- Large enterprises (>1,500 FTE) — likely have dedicated procurement teams
- Bootstrapped startups (<200 FTE) — may lack budget authority
- Single-office companies — insufficient distributed team pain point
- Highly transactional responses (one-word replies) — unlikely to engage deeply

### 1.2 Call Scheduling Best Practices

**Proposed timing:**
- Reach out for scheduling within 24 hours of positive response
- Offer 3 time slots in the next 3–5 business days
- Propose 30–45 minute calls (default to 30 min; extend only if prospect asks)
- Schedule for early-mid week (Tue–Thu) to avoid end-of-week decision fatigue

## Part 2: Discovery Call Script & Validation Framework

### 2.1 Call Structure (30–45 minutes total)

```
0–2 min:   Warm intro + rapport building
2–5 min:   Understand current swag workflow & pain points
5–15 min:  Live demo (domain input → brand extraction → storefront)
15–25 min: Pricing discussion + $24K WTP signal
25–35 min: Pilot logistics + timeline + objections
35–45 min: Close + next steps
```

### 2.2 Opening (0–2 minutes)

**Purpose:** Build rapport, frame the call, set expectations

**Script:**

> "Hey [Name], thanks for taking the time today! I'm excited to learn more about how [Company] currently approaches branded merchandise and swag.
>
> Here's what I'm thinking for the next 30 minutes: First, I'd love to understand your current workflow — what works well and what's painful. Then I'm going to show you how Branded Fit works by generating a live preview of [Company]'s branded apparel catalog using your actual branding. Finally, we'll talk about how this could fit into your budget and whether a pilot makes sense. Sound good?"

**Validation point:** Confirm they have 30 min and are in a place where we can talk openly.

---

### 2.3 Current Workflow & Pain Points (2–5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Validate persona fit, identify pain points, and create hooks for demo

**Key Questions:**

1. **Persona fit question:**
   > "Walk me through your current swag workflow. Who owns this — is it you, or do you have someone else on your team managing it?"
   - **Listen for:** Confirms they own the decision (persona fit: YES if they say "it's me" or "I oversee it")
   - **Red flag:** "Our procurement team handles this" → may not have buying authority

2. **Pain point #1 (lead time):**
   > "How long does it typically take from the moment you decide 'we need company swag' to the moment employees actually receive it?"
   - **Listen for:** 6–8 weeks, unpredictable timelines, rush fees, last-minute scrambles
   - **Follow-up:** "What's the biggest frustration with the current timeline?"

3. **Pain point #2 (brand accuracy):**
   > "How do you currently ensure that the swag actually matches your company's brand — colors, logo placement, overall vibe?"
   - **Listen for:** Manual review, back-and-forth with vendors, "we have to QA every mockup"
   - **Follow-up:** "Has brand accuracy ever been a problem — like, did you ship something and it didn't quite look right?"

4. **Pain point #3 (cost & budget):**
   > "How much do you currently spend on company swag in a year?"
   - **Listen for:** Any number (note it for later comparison to $24K)
   - **Follow-up:** "If you could reduce that by 20–30% while improving speed and quality, would that be valuable?"

5. **Pain point #4 (coordination):**
   > "Who else is involved in the swag decision — do you work with Marketing, Design, Finance?"
   - **Listen for:** Multiple stakeholders, approval cycles, back-and-forth
   - **Follow-up:** "How many rounds of feedback does it typically take before you're happy with the final design?"

**Tracking:** Note 1–2 most painful points for later reference in demo/close.

### 2.4 Live Product Demo (5–15 minutes)

**Purpose:** Show brand extraction accuracy, <10-min provisioning, and storefront quality. Embed real-time validation questions.

**Setup (before call):**
- Have Branded Fit Command Console open in a browser tab
- Have a similar company's domain pre-filled (e.g., Ramp, Notion, or Vanta)
- Explain: "I've already pulled your company domain; let me show you what happens when we extract your branding and generate a storefront."

**Demo flow:**

```
STEP 1: BRAND EXTRACTION (2 min)
---
Live action: Submit domain → Show Brandfetch extraction results

Narration:
"So what's happening here is we're connecting to Brandfetch, which pulls your 
company's visual brand assets directly from your website — colors, logos, fonts, 
imagery. This is fully automated; you don't need to upload anything manually."

Live results display:
- Primary colors (e.g., #003DA5, #FF6B00)
- Logo variations (horizontal, vertical, icon)
- Typography (brand fonts)
- Brand imagery (hero images from site)

VALIDATION QUESTION (embed here):
"As I'm pulling this, rate what you see: does this capture your brand accurately? 
1–5 scale, where 5 is 'perfect' and 1 is 'way off'?"

→ LISTEN FOR: 4–5 is CONFIRM, 2–3 is CAUTION (may indicate API limitations)

STEP 2: STOREFRONT GENERATION (2–3 min)
---
Live action: Show spinning loader → Display auto-generated product catalog

Narration:
"While that's loading, what's happening behind the scenes is we're applying your 
brand to our product catalog. We work with Printify, which has 300+ on-demand 
manufacturers. So within seconds, your employees will see a branded store 
pre-populated with hoodies, t-shirts, hats, water bottles — all in your colors, 
with your logo."

Live results display:
- Product grid (10–15 products)
- Each product showing mockup with brand applied
- Brand colors visible on every product
- Logo placement consistent

VALIDATION QUESTION (embed here):
"Looking at these mockups, how accurate is the color reproduction? Does this 
match what you'd expect from your brand guidelines?"

→ LISTEN FOR: Recognition ("yes, those are our colors"), hesitation (color 
mismatch), excitement (strong brand signal)

STEP 3: PROVISIONING TIME (1–2 min)
---
Live action: Check timestamp from domain submission to storefront display

Narration:
"So from the moment I entered your domain to right now, we've extracted your 
brand and built this entire storefront. [Check elapsed time]. That's [X] minutes. 
Our target is under 10 minutes, and we hit it. Traditional agencies? 4–8 weeks."

VALIDATION QUESTION:
"If you could go from 'we need swag' to 'employees can start ordering' in under 
10 minutes instead of 4–8 weeks, how would that change how you approach branded 
merchandise?"

→ LISTEN FOR: Excitement ("we'd do this way more often"), skepticism 
("feels too fast, what's the catch"), practical interest ("we'd need to try it")

STEP 4: STOREFRONT OPERATION (1–2 min)
---
Live narration (no action needed):
"Once this goes live, your employees log in, order what they want — quantity, 
size, color. No inventory costs for you. You only pay per unit when they order. 
We handle the fulfillment, shipping, and QA. You get a dashboard showing who 
ordered what, costs per employee, spending trends."

VALIDATION QUESTION:
"Does the employee self-service model make sense for your company, or would you 
prefer a more curated approach where you select specific items?"

→ LISTEN FOR: Self-service fit (good), curated preference (note for feature request)
```

**Total demo time: 5–8 min. Remaining 2–7 min for prospect questions/reactions.**

### 2.5 Pricing Discussion & WTP Signal (15–25 minutes)

**Purpose:** Directly test $24K annual commitment willingness. Establish baseline for pricing feedback.

**Context-setting (1 min):**

> "So here's how we price this. We have three tiers: Starter, Growth, and Enterprise. 
> Most companies in your size range land in Growth. Let me walk you through what each includes."

**Pricing breakdown (2–3 min):**

| Tier | Annual | Features | Ideal for |
|------|--------|----------|-----------|
| **Starter** | $4,800 | 1–2 campaigns/year, <500 products, basic support | Small/distributed teams |
| **Growth** | $24,000 | Unlimited campaigns, unlimited products, priority support | 200–1,500 FTE |
| **Enterprise** | Custom | White-label, SLA, dedicated account manager | 1,500+ FTE or custom needs |

**Narration:**

> "Growth is $24K per year, which breaks down to $2K per month. That includes unlimited 
> brand refreshes, unlimited product drops, and priority support. Most companies in your 
> size range use this for 2–4 major campaigns per year — like onboarding, holidays, 
> anniversaries — plus ad-hoc refreshes."

**DIRECT WTP QUESTION:**

> "Given what you've seen today — the speed, the brand accuracy, the employee self-service 
> model — would you consider a $24K annual commitment for unlimited campaigns?"

**Listen for response type:**

1. **Strong YES (WTP CONFIRMED):**
   - "Yeah, that makes sense for what we do"
   - "We probably spend more than that now, so this would actually save us money"
   - "I could definitely get that approved"
   - → **RECORD:** WTP = $24K, confidence = HIGH

2. **Conditional YES (WTP NEGOTIABLE):**
   - "It depends on results from a pilot"
   - "Can we start with Starter and upgrade?"
   - "Maybe $20K?"
   - → **RECORD:** WTP = $18-24K, confidence = MEDIUM

3. **NO or PAUSE (WTP LOW):**
   - "That's above our current budget"
   - "We'd need to see it in action first"
   - "Our CFO would need to sign off, and they're budget-conscious"
   - → **RECORD:** WTP = <$18K or uncertain, confidence = LOW

**Follow-up questions (if they hesitate):**

> "What would be a more comfortable price point for you to start with?"

> "If we could prove ROI in a pilot, would the annual commitment make sense?"

> "What's your current annual swag spend? That'll help me understand the comparison."

### 2.6 Pilot Offer & Timeline (25–35 minutes)

**Purpose:** Gauge willingness to move from discovery to paid pilot. Establish timeline to decision.

**Pilot offer framing (2 min):**

> "Here's what I'd suggest: Let's run a Brand Drop Pilot. That's $4,800, covers a single 
> campaign — maybe an onboarding kit or holiday gift — 50 units, 14-day fulfillment and 
> delivery. You'll see the full workflow, understand costs, and have real product in hand 
> to show the team.
>
> At the end, we'll debrief. If you love it, we can talk about moving to Growth. If not, 
> no hard feelings — you got a great onboarding kit and data to inform your next move."

**DIRECT PILOT QUESTION:**

> "Would you be interested in running this pilot in the next 30 days?"

**Listen for response type:**

1. **STRONG YES (PILOT COMMITTED):**
   - "Absolutely, let's do it"
   - "I can get budget approved this week"
   - → **RECORD:** Pilot intent = YES, timeline = <2 weeks

2. **CONDITIONAL YES (PILOT PROBABLE):**
   - "I need to check with my manager/Finance"
   - "Let me get budget approval, but I'm excited"
   - "Can we start at the end of June?"
   - → **RECORD:** Pilot intent = LIKELY, timeline = 2–4 weeks

3. **MAYBE (PILOT UNCERTAIN):**
   - "I want to see more proof"
   - "Can we schedule another call after I present internally?"
   - "I like it, but need to align with leadership"
   - → **RECORD:** Pilot intent = MAYBE, timeline = >4 weeks

4. **NO (PILOT REJECTED):**
   - "Too expensive for a pilot"
   - "We're not ready yet"
   - "Let me revisit this in Q3"
   - → **RECORD:** Pilot intent = NO, timeline = defer

**If MAYBE or NO, probe for objections:**

> "What would need to happen for you to feel comfortable moving forward with a pilot?"

> "Is there a different pilot size or price point that would work better?"

> "What's the main blocker right now — budget, internal buy-in, feature gaps?"

**Document all objections (see Section 3.1 below).**

---

### 2.7 Closing & Next Steps (35–45 minutes)

**Recap (2 min):**

> "So to recap: You're looking to [pain point]. We can solve that with [solution]. 
> You're interested in a pilot [if TRUE] / want to explore further [if MAYBE]. Here's 
> what I'm going to do, and here's what I need from you."

**For STRONG YES / COMMITTED pilots:**

> "I'm going to send you a pilot agreement and onboarding instructions within 24 hours. 
> You'll receive a link to set up your brand in our platform, and we'll kick off 
> production on [DATE]. Timeline to delivery is 14 days. Sound good?"

**For CONDITIONAL YES / LIKELY pilots:**

> "You mentioned needing to check with [Manager/Finance]. Here's what I'll do: I'll send 
> you a one-pager summarizing today's conversation, the pilot pricing, and ROI assumptions. 
> You can use that in your conversation with them. Let's plan a follow-up call for [DATE] 
> to confirm once you've gotten alignment."

**For MAYBE / UNCERTAIN:**

> "I get it — you want to explore more internally. Here's my recommendation: Take a few 
> days, discuss with your team, and let me know if you have questions. In the meantime, 
> I'll send over some case studies and a pricing/FAQ document. Let's reconnect on [DATE] 
> and see if there are any other objections we can tackle."

**For NO / DEFERRED:**

> "Totally understand. This might be the right time for you later. Here's what I'll do: 
> I'll stay in touch every 6–8 weeks. If something changes — budget, team priorities, 
> new hire — reach out. And if you think of anyone else who might find this useful, 
> I'd love a warm intro."

**Collect contact information (1 min):**

- Confirm email and phone
- Ask for manager's name (if applicable) for future follow-up
- Ask: "Is there anyone else on your team I should loop in as we progress?"

## Part 6: Risk Mitigation & Contingencies

### Risk: Prospect cancels or no-shows

**Mitigation:**
- Send calendar reminder 24 hours before
- Call/text 10 minutes before if no response
- Have backup prospects queued for same day if possible
- Reschedule with flexibility on dates/times

### Risk: Demo fails or is slow

**Mitigation:**
- Test Command Console 1 hour before each call
- Have backup (pre-recorded demo video) ready
- Use similar-company domain that you know works quickly
- Frame slowness as "typical first-time load" — not a deal-breaker

### Risk: Prospect challenges pricing midway

**Mitigation:**
- Don't defend price on the call; instead ask: "What would be a comfortable starting point?"
- Pivot to pilot ($4,800) as lower-friction entry
- Offer: "Let's see results on pilot, then reassess annual commitment"
- Reference WTP data from other calls (if positive)

### Risk: Feature gap blocks pilot commitment

**Mitigation:**
- Document required feature + timeline to build
- Offer interim workaround (e.g., manual integration, post-launch enhancement)
- Set expectation: "We can build that in Q3, or we can ship pilot without it in Q2"
- Escalate to product team immediately

### Risk: Can't reach decision-maker

**Mitigation:**
- Ask on discovery call: "Who else needs to approve this? Can you introduce us?"
- Offer to present to executive team (VP Finance, VP People, CEO)
- Provide internal champion (the person you spoke with) with one-pager + talking points
- Schedule 2nd call once internal alignment happens

- [ ] 3 calls completed, but only 1 persona fit confirmed
- [ ] 0 committed pilots, but ≥2 likely/maybe
- [ ] WTP signal splits 50/50 between $24K and <$18K
- [ ] 1–2 deal-blocking objections recur across calls
- [ ] Demo resonated but pricing was biggest objection

**Decision:** **PIVOT** — Adjust messaging to emphasize speed/quality over automation. Offer tiered pricing ($12K Starter → $24K Growth). Fix most-mentioned feature gaps before next outreach wave.

### REFUTE Criteria (Red light to reassess)

- [ ] 3+ calls completed, but <50% persona fit
- [ ] 0 pilots, 0 maybes — all rejections
- [ ] WTP signal all <$15K
- [ ] ≥75% of prospects raised deal-blocking objections
- [ ] Demo flopped on brand accuracy or speed

**Decision:** **NO-GO** — Pause outreach. Conduct root-cause analysis:
  - Does product need significant improvement? (Reassess Step 7 core mechanic)
  - Does positioning miss the mark? (Rewrite messaging)
  - Is the persona wrong? (Revisit Step 9 market segmentation)
  - Is pricing fundamentally misaligned? (Revisit Step 16 pricing)
