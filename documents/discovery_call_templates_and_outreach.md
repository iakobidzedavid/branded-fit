## Part C: Discovery Call Execution Script (30–40 minutes)

**Pre-Call (5 min before):**
- [ ] Review prospect's company, role, warm-intro context
- [ ] Test audio/video; have water nearby
- [ ] Open tracker template; have note-taking app ready
- [ ] Set phone to Do Not Disturb

**Opening (0–2 min)**
- [ ] "Hi [First Name], thanks so much for making time today."
- [ ] **Permission check:** "I've got 25 minutes blocked—does that still work for you?"
- [ ] **Tone:** Warm, founder energy. Not salesy. Peer-to-peer.

**Warm-Up & Context (2–4 min)**
- [ ] "I appreciate [Referrer Name] connecting us" OR "Love that you reached out."
- [ ] **Set expectation:** "Today is just me learning about how you think about branded merchandise. No pitch. If it doesn't make sense, that's totally fine."
- [ ] **Build credibility:** "We've been working with teams like [Example Company] on this exact problem, and I've learned a ton."

**Current State Discovery (4–10 min) — 70% LISTENING**
- [ ] "Walk me through your current swag workflow. Where does it break down?"
- [ ] Listen for pain:
  - Vendor responsiveness / timelines
  - Quality / brand-fidelity issues
  - Cost unpredictability
  - Vendor relationship friction
- [ ] **Follow-up:** "What's the cost / time impact of that problem?"
- [ ] Map their pains to Branded Fit's 3 pillars: **Speed, Brand Fidelity, On-Demand Fulfillment**

**Value-Prop Alignment (10–16 min)**
- [ ] "Based on what you said, here's what we'd do differently..."
- [ ] **Show 90-second loom / mockup** (if available): [Link to mockup video]
- [ ] Walk through:
  - Brand-extraction process (Brandfetch API)
  - Mockup preview + approval gates
  - 14-day fulfillment SLA
  - Per-unit pricing (no setup fees)
- [ ] **Credibility anchors:**
  - "We've worked with [Reference Company] on this; happy to intro you if helpful."
  - "NPS from pilots is running [X]."
  - "Most vendors take 6–8 weeks; we promise 14 days or full refund."

**Van Westendorp Pricing Ladder (16–24 min) — CRITICAL**
- [ ] "I want to understand your comfort with pricing. I'm going to throw four price points at you, and tell me your gut reaction. No overthinking."
- [ ] For **each anchor**, ask:
  - "At what price would Branded Fit be **too cheap to be good**?" (quality concern)
    - *Listen for: Below $[X], they'd worry about corners cut*
    - *Record as $2K anchor perception*
  - "At what price would it be **too expensive**?" (not worth it)
    - *Listen for: Above $[X], ROI doesn't make sense*
    - *Record as $36K anchor perception*
  - "At what price would you **seriously consider** a pilot?" (sweet spot)
    - *Listen for: Their acceptable range, e.g., $4K–$6K*
    - *Record as $4.8K pilot perception*
  - "What's your gut on **$24K annually** for a full Growth tier?" (annual pricing)
    - *Listen for: Feasibility, budget viability*

- [ ] **Record exactly:**
  - Their verbatim words on each anchor
  - Why they said that (budget constraint? value perception? vendor comparison?)

**Objection Handling (24–29 min) — ANTICIPATE + LISTEN**
- [ ] "Before we wrap, what concerns do you have? What would make you hesitate?"
- [ ] Common objections to explore:
  1. "We already have a vendor relationship" → *"How painful is it to switch? What would make the switch worth it?"*
  2. "How do you guarantee brand accuracy?" → *"What's happened in the past?"*
  3. "Can you handle [specific need: multi-brand, enterprise scale, etc.]?" → *"What's your constraint?"*
  4. "What's your procurement process?" → *"PO required? IT security review? Budget cycles?"*

- [ ] **For each objection:**
  - Record the objection verbatim
  - Understand the underlying pain (not just the surface objection)
  - Provide specific counter-evidence: case studies, guarantees, data

**Pilot Intent (29–33 min)**
- [ ] "If we could get this right for you, would a **$4,800 pilot with 50 units** be something you'd want to test?"
  - [ ] Listen for:
    - "Yes, let's do it" → Confidence 5
    - "Yes, but only if [condition]" → Confidence 4
    - "Maybe, depends on [X]" → Confidence 3
    - "Not right now, but maybe later" → Confidence 2
    - "No, not a fit" → Confidence 1

- [ ] If hesitant: *"What would need to change for you to say yes?"*
- [ ] If yes: *"Rough timeline—when could you get this approved?"*

**Capture Verbatim Quotes (33–37 min)**
- [ ] "I want to make sure I capture how you think about this. A few quick questions:"
- [ ] "In your words, why does **speed** matter for swag?"
  - *Record verbatim quote*
- [ ] "What would **brand-fidelity guarantee** mean to your team?"
  - *Record verbatim quote*
- [ ] "How important is **on-demand fulfillment**—can you do just-in-time orders?"
  - *Record verbatim quote*
- [ ] Any other memorable comment? *Record it.*

**Close & Next Steps (37–40 min)**
- [ ] "Here's what I'm thinking we do next..."
  - If **Pilot Intent = Yes:** "I'll send you a draft SOW by [date]. Rough timeline?"
  - If **Pilot Intent = Maybe:** "Let me send you two case studies from similar companies. When can we reconnect—next [day]?"
  - If **Pilot Intent = No:** "Totally understand. If things change or you know someone else this might fit, happy to help. Thanks for the time."

- [ ] **Set explicit next-action date.**
- [ ] "Great conversation. I'm going to send you [deliverable] by [date]."

**Post-Call (5 min after)**
- [ ] [ ] Log all data into tracker immediately while fresh
- [ ] [ ] Update their prospect record with call outcome
- [ ] [ ] Flag pilot-intent signals for prioritization
- [ ] [ ] Schedule follow-up action (SOW send, case study follow-up, etc.)

## Part D: Objection Handling Playbook

### Objection 1: "We already have a vendor relationship"

**Prospect's Real Fear:**
- Switching costs (renegotiation, onboarding)
- Political friction (current vendor is "owned" by someone)
- Fear of unknown (current vendor is mediocre but stable)

**Our Counter:**
- "I get it. How much of that friction is from the vendor, and how much is just the hassle of switching?"
- **Case study anchor:** "We migrated [Company X] away from [Vendor] in 3 weeks. We handle brand asset transfer, pricing negotiation, even introductions to our payment team."
- **Risk removal:** "What if we started with a small pilot—say 50 units—so you test us before full migration?"
- **Outcome:** Their buy-in is conditional on us proving **speed + quality** in a low-risk trial.

---

### Objection 2: "How do you guarantee brand accuracy?"

**Prospect's Real Fear:**
- Previous vendor shipped wrong colors, logos, fonts
- Brand is sacred (especially in fast-growth startups)
- They don't want to manage quality control

**Our Counter:**
- "That's the exact problem we solve. Here's how: [explain Brandfetch + manual verification gates]."
- **Show proof:** "On every order, we extract brand specs, generate mockup, get your sign-off, *then* go to production. If it's wrong, we reprint at no cost."
- **Data:** "[X]% of our pilot customers gave us 5-star fidelity scores; [Y] said we were better than their previous vendor."
- **Outcome:** Move them from skepticism to curiosity about the process.

---

### Objection 3: "Can you handle [multi-brand / enterprise scale / personalization]?"

**Prospect's Real Fear:**
- They have a complex requirement (multiple portfolio companies, SKU explosion, personalization)
- They think we're too basic

**Our Counter:**
- "Yes, and here's how we do it: [specific example]. We currently support [Company A] with [X] brands, [Company B] with [Y] SKUs, etc."
- **Proof of scale:** "Our backend handles [Z] orders/month; we're not a freelance service."
- **Outcome:** Demonstrate we understand their complexity and have the infrastructure.

---

### Objection 4: "What's your procurement process? (PO, terms, security review)"

**Prospect's Real Fear:**
- Bureaucracy will slow us down
- We don't fit their vendor management workflow

**Our Counter:**
- "Great question. What does your process look like?" *(Let them explain.)*
- "We can work within [X timeframe, payment terms, security framework]. Here's what we typically do: [explain our standard]."
- **Flexibility:** "If there's a specific requirement (IT security, data residency, etc.), let's talk—we've been flexible for other customers."
- **Outcome:** Reduce perceived friction; set clear expectations.
