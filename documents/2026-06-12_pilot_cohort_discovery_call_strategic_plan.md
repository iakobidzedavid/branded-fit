# Pilot Cohort Discovery Call Strategic Plan
## Branded Fit: Annual Contract Validation & Y2 Roadmap Alignment
**Date:** June 12, 2026  
**Version:** 1.0 (Production-Ready)  
**Objective:** Validate annual contract intent, pricing acceptance at $24K/yr Growth tier, identify top 3 product gaps, and synthesize findings into Y2 GTM messaging  
**Target Customers:** Vanta, Linear, Census, Hex, Mercury (5 paid Brand Drop Pilot customers)

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This strategic plan establishes the framework for conducting 5 structured discovery calls (1 per customer, 45–60 minutes each) with Branded Fit's paid pilot cohort. These calls are critical dependencies for:

1. **Step 21 Assumption Validation (A6: Y2 NRR & Retention)** — Measure intent to convert from $4.8K pilot to $24K annual Growth contract
2. **Step 23 Proof-of-Concept (Annual Conversion Intent)** — Validate the underlying assumption that 4/5 pilots will convert to recurring Growth contracts by EOY
3. **GTM Messaging Readiness (Y2 Positioning)** — Ground Year 2 outreach positioning in real customer voice, gaps, and priorities

**Methodology:** Parallel Assumption Validation framework with 8–10 open-ended discovery questions per call, synthesized into 1-page customer briefs post-call, consolidated into a final "Pilot Cohort Discovery Synthesis" report with:
- Aggregate NPS score
- Top 3 common product requests
- Conversion-to-annual intent probability (%)
- Recommended messaging shifts for Year 2 outreach
- Risk flags (pricing resistance, feature gaps, churn signals)

**Success Metrics:**
- All 5 calls completed within 6 business days (June 12–19)
- 100% discovery call completion rate
- ≥3 clear, actionable product requests per call
- Annual contract conversion intent classified for each customer (Yes/Maybe/No/Abstain)
- Aggregate NPS ≥7.5/10 (indicates healthy brand perception)
- Zero calls canceled or rescheduled after initial booking

### 1.2 Parallel Assumption Validation Framework

The discovery call is designed to validate **three critical assumptions** with equal weight:

| Assumption | Definition | Success Threshold | Signal to Measure |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|------------------|
| **A1: Pricing Acceptance** | Prospect accepts $24K/yr Growth tier as "fair value" | ≥60% of cohort rates as "fair" or "cheap" on Van Westendorp ladder | Van Westendorp 4-point anchors: $2K (cheap) / $4.8K (bargain) / $24K (fair) / $36K (expensive) |
| **A2: Brand Fidelity Importance** | Brand fidelity (mockup quality, color accuracy) is top-3 buying criterion | ≥60% cite brand fidelity in top 3 criteria | Ranking of 8+ buying criteria; fidelity placement; quote strength |
| **A3: Annual Conversion Intent** | ≥4/5 pilots convert from Brand Drop Pilot ($4.8K) to Growth contract ($24K) by EOY | ≥80% express "Yes" or "Strong Maybe" conversion intent | Direct: "If we build X, would you commit to Growth contract by EOY?" + scenario-based followup |

**Scoring Rubric:**
- **CONFIRM:** 2+ customers explicitly agree, zero disagree
- **GRAY:** Mixed signals (1 agree, 1 disagree, or 2+ say "maybe")
- **REFUTE:** 2+ customers explicitly disagree; stated price sensitivity or deprioritized feature

## SECTION 2: DISCOVERY CALL STRUCTURE & SCRIPT

### 2.1 Call Scheduling & Logistics

**Scheduling Requirements:**
- Target window: June 12–19, 2026 (6 business days)
- Duration: 45–60 minutes
- Format: Video call (Zoom/Google Meet) with permission to record + transcribe
- Attendee from Branded Fit: 1 founder or founding team member (establishes credibility + peer-to-peer tone)
- Attendee from customer: Decision maker listed in "Pre-Call Research" (avoid group calls to maximize candor)
- Recording: Explicitly request permission at call start; "I'd like to record so I can capture your feedback accurately and share with our product team."

**Confirmation Checklist:**
- [ ] Calendar invite sent 5 days before call with Zoom link
- [ ] Reminder email sent 24 hours before
- [ ] Pre-call research document prepared (1 page per customer)
- [ ] Discovery script printed or open on secondary screen
- [ ] Recording device tested + Zoom recording enabled
- [ ] Transcript note-taking template open (see Section 2.4)
- [ ] Demo environment staged (live product link ready if needed)

### 2.2 Opening (0–2 minutes)

**Objective:** Establish credibility, reference warm intro (if applicable), ask permission to record, set agenda.

**Script:**

> "Thanks so much for jumping on. I'm [Name] from Branded Fit. I want to dive into how the Brand Drop Pilot has gone so far, understand where we're nailing it and where we might be missing the mark, and get your early read on whether a Growth contract makes sense for you later this year. Sound good?
> 
> I'd like to record this so I can make sure I capture your feedback accurately and share key takeaways with our product team. Is that okay?
> 
> We'll go for about 45 minutes. I'm going to ask a lot of open-ended questions—no wrong answers here. Your honest feedback, even if it's critical, is super valuable for us."

**Tone:** Founder energy, conversational, peer-to-peer, not scripted-sounding. Aim for 70% listening, 30% talking.

### 2.3 Discovery Questions (2–40 minutes)

**Framework:** Follow a 5-phase discovery sequence. Ask one question, then listen. Use pauses to encourage deeper reflection.

#### Phase 1: Current Swag Operations (4–6 minutes)

*Goal: Understand baseline workflow, pain points, current solution*

**Q1. Workflow Mapping**
> "Walk me through your current swag process today. How do you think about it? Who owns it? What's the timeline look like when you need to turn something around?"

*Listen for:* Operational burden, decision-maker distribution, procurement cycle, pain points (slow turnarounds, brand consistency, cost). *Probe:* "Who else is involved in that approval?" "How long does a typical request take end-to-end?"

**Q2. Brand Consistency Challenges** (if not surfaced in Q1)
> "When you're pulling together swag, how do you make sure it actually matches your brand? What does that look like? Any frustrations there?"

*Listen for:* Vendor confusion, mockup rejection cycles, color/logo accuracy concerns, rework. *Probe:* "Have you had to reject designs?" "What's the cost of a do-over?"

#### Phase 2: Van Westendorp Willingness-to-Pay (6–8 minutes)

*Goal: Quantify pricing acceptance with 4-point anchors*

> "Now let's talk about pricing. Thinking about an annual, all-you-can-use swag service where you could request mockups, get brand-fidelity guarantees, and have fulfillment handled—what would feel like a bargain to you?"

*Record verbatim answer, then anchor:*

> "And what would feel cheap—like, suspiciously cheap?"

> "At what price would it feel expensive, like you'd want to negotiate?"

> "And at what price would it be so expensive you wouldn't even consider it?"

*Scoring:*
- If $24K falls between "fair" and "cheap" anchors → **CONFIRM pricing acceptance**
- If $24K falls between "expensive" and "fair" → **GRAY**
- If $24K exceeds "expensive" anchor → **REFUTE**

*Probe if unclear:* "So if we offered $24K per year, all-inclusive, would that feel like good value given what you're getting?"

#### Phase 3: Top 3 Buying Criteria (6–8 minutes)

*Goal: Validate brand fidelity as top-3 criterion; surface other priorities*

> "When you're evaluating a swag provider, what matters most? Give me your top 3–5 things."

*Listen and rank as they speak. Typical responses: speed / turnaround, brand fidelity, price, vendor reliability, fulfillment quality, mockup quality, ease of use, customization, supplier relationships, compliance/audits.*

> "Out of those, which one would be a true deal-breaker if we got it wrong?"

*Probe:* "You mentioned [criterion]. Can you give me a specific example of what 'good' looks like?"

*Scoring:*
- Brand fidelity or mockup quality ranked #1–2 for ≥4/5 customers → **CONFIRM**
- Mixed rankings across cohort → **GRAY**
- Unanimously ranked #4+ → **REFUTE**

#### Phase 4: Product Requests & Feature Gaps (8–10 minutes)

*Goal: Identify top 3 product gaps per customer; prioritize for Y2 roadmap*

> "Where do you think we're missing the mark? What would make this service significantly better for you?"

*Listen without interrupting. Take verbatim notes. Common themes: bulk ordering UX, API integration, approval workflows, variant management, reorder templates, multi-brand support, CSM support, customer portal, order history/analytics, compliance/audit trails.*

> "Of those, which three would have the biggest impact on your team's workflow?"

*Probe:* "How often would you use that feature?" "Would that change how you think about annual adoption?"

#### Phase 5: Conversion Intent & NPS (8–10 minutes)

*Goal: Classify conversion likelihood; measure NPS*

> "Let's zoom out. We're thinking about a Growth tier—basically the pilot on steroids with some extra features and dedicated support. If we delivered on [top 2 product requests], would you be interested in signing an annual contract by end of year? What would need to change?"

*Listen for:* Explicit yes/maybe/no, conditional requirements, budget constraints, procurement concerns, competing priorities.

*Score:*
- **"Yes, if you build X"** → Count as "Strong Maybe"
- **"Definitely, we're in"** → Count as "Yes"
- **"We'd need to see it in action"** → Count as "Maybe"
- **"Pricing doesn't work" or "Not a priority"** → Count as "No"

> "Last question: on a scale of 0–10, how likely are you to recommend Branded Fit to a peer in your network—someone at a similar company with a similar swag challenge?"

*Record score. Probe if ≤6:* "What would get you to a 7 or 8?"

### 2.4 Closing (40–45 minutes)

**Objective:** Confirm next steps, recap action items, thank the customer.

**Script:**

> "This has been super helpful. Let me recap what I heard: [2–3 key themes]. We're going to dig into [product request #1 and #2] with the team and get back to you with a rough roadmap and timeline. In the meantime, keep using the pilot, and if something comes up or you have other feedback, shoot me an email."

> "We'll likely want to do a quick follow-up check-in in late July, before we finalize pricing and contract terms for Growth. Cool?"

> "Thanks so much. This really helps us build the right thing."

## SECTION 9: RISK MITIGATION & CONTINGENCIES

### Risk 1: Call Cancellation / Reschedule

**Probability:** Medium (1–2 customers may reschedule)

**Mitigation:**
- Book calls 5 days in advance (not last-minute)
- Confirm 24 hours before with reminder email
- Offer 3–4 time slots to minimize friction
- If cancellation occurs, reschedule within 48 hours

**Fallback:** If customer unavailable before June 19, mark brief as "Deferred" and conduct async survey (email questionnaire) as secondary option.

### Risk 2: Customer Reluctance / Low Candor

**Probability:** Low-Medium (customer may be guarded if unsatisfied)

**Mitigation:**
- Start call with explicit: "Your honest feedback, even if critical, is valuable. There are no wrong answers."
- Listen more, pitch less (70/30 ratio)
- Use open-ended follow-ups ("Tell me more about that") to encourage depth
- Avoid confrontation on objections; instead, probe curiosity ("Why does that feel expensive to you?")

**Signal:** If customer offers only generic responses (≤20 min of substantive discussion), flag as "Low candor" and consider brief incomplete until clarifications sought.

### Risk 3: Inconclusive Conversion Intent

**Probability:** Medium (4/5 customers may say "maybe" or express conditions)

**Mitigation:**
- In-call follow-up: "What would move a 'maybe' to a 'yes'?" (get specific conditions)
- Post-call: If unclear, send follow-up email asking to rank: "How likely is Growth contract by EOY? (1=Very unlikely, 10=Certain)"
- Use conditions as roadmap input, not blocker (e.g., "Yes if we ship [feature]")

### Risk 4: Conflicting Customer Feedback (No Clear Top 3 Requests)

**Probability:** Low-Medium

**Mitigation:**
- Rank by frequency first (most customers mention = priority)
- Tiebreaker: severity (would it block conversion? would it improve NPS?)
- Tiebreaker: effort (high-impact, lower-effort requests prioritized)
- If still unclear, discuss with product team to weigh against existing roadmap

## SECTION 10: APPENDIX & REFERENCE MATERIALS

### Appendix A: Van Westendorp Pricing Ladder Example

Customer answer: "I'd say $2K is a bargain, $5K is fair, $15K feels expensive, $25K is way too much."

```
$2K  ← Cheap (suspiciously low-priced)
$5K  ← Fair (good value)
$15K ← Expensive (sticker shock)
$25K ← Too expensive (no negotiation)
```

**Scoring:** $24K falls between "Expensive" ($15K) and "Too Expensive" ($25K), indicating **GRAY** pricing acceptance. Follow-up: "So $24K would feel pricey, but possible?" → Probe willingness to negotiate.
