# Discovery Call Objection Handling Playbook
**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 2026-06-15  
**Purpose:** Proven counters to top objections that will arise during discovery calls

---

## Objection Handling Framework

### The Pattern
1. **Listen fully** (don't interrupt)
2. **Validate the concern** (show you understand)
3. **Reframe** (connect to their pain point)
4. **Provide counter** (social proof, case study, mechanism)
5. **Check understanding** (confirm reframe landed)

---

## Top 5 Predicted Objections & Counters

### OBJECTION #1: "Can you really handle our brand guidelines exactly?"
**Risk Level:** ⚠️ MEDIUM (Can be a deal-blocker if not handled well)  
**Why it comes up:** People Ops teams have brand police. Exact color match matters.

**Listen For:**
- "Our CMO is really strict about brand."
- "We had a bad experience with [vendor] doing brand wrong."
- "The hex codes have to be perfect."

**Validation:**
"I hear you. Brands are a big deal, and you're right—off-brand swag is worse than no swag."

**Reframe:**
"Here's the thing: we don't guess. We extract your actual brand from your website using Brandfetch API. It reads your Figma, CSS, and brand assets. Then we show you a mockup before manufacturing. You approve or request changes. It's like working with a designer, except it takes 10 minutes instead of 3 weeks."

**Social Proof Counter:**
"We've run this with [peer company] and [peer company #2]. Their brand team approved mockups 95% of the time on first pass. The 5% that needed tweaks? We iterate in 24 hours."

**Specific Mechanism:**
"Show mockup in demo" → "See how the colors pull from your site?" → "You have approval power before we manufacture."

**Check Understanding:**
"Does seeing the mockup before we order help with the brand guardrail concern?"

### OBJECTION #2: "This seems cool, but we don't have budget for another tool."
**Risk Level:** 🔴 HIGH (Most common budget objection)  
**Why it comes up:** IT/Procurement gatekeeping. Budgets are locked.

**Listen For:**
- "Our swag budget is already allocated."
- "We'd need to find room in the People Ops budget."
- "What's the ROI?"

**Validation:**
"I get it. You've probably got that budget locked in with a vendor already."

**Reframe:**
"Here's how most teams think about it: You're already spending $X on swag every quarter. The question isn't whether you have budget—it's whether you're getting the most value from it. We're not *adding* cost; we're *replacing* cost with a better way."

**ROI Counter (Quantify First):**
"Let's do the math: How many new hires per quarter? Let's say 50. You're probably spending $50-200 per onboarding kit. That's $2.5-10K per quarter already. Now, how many of those kits are a miss? Like, wrong size, wrong colors, employees don't love them? I'd guess 10-20% are underwhelming."

"With our approach, employees *choose* colors and sizes. Our pilot data shows this lifts engagement by 30%. So instead of replacing one vendor with another, you're improving NPS at the same cost level."

**Specific Offer:**
"Here's the move: Do a $4,800 Brand Drop Pilot for your next cohort (one month). That's zero new budget—just reallocate from your next vendor order. If your employees love it, we talk about the full-year plan."

**Check Understanding:**
"Does doing one small pilot before committing to a full tool make sense?"

### OBJECTION #3: "We already use [SwagUp / Bonusly / Printful / etc.]—why switch?"
**Risk Level:** ⚠️ MEDIUM (Switching-cost friction)  
**Why it comes up:** Existing vendor relationships, setup costs, data migration.

**Listen For:**
- "SwagUp already handles our gifting."
- "We just implemented Bonusly 6 months ago."
- "We don't want to disrupt our current process."

**Validation:**
"Totally fair. Switching tools is annoying. I'm not here to rip out your current setup."

**Reframe:**
"We're actually not a replacement for those tools. SwagUp does *occasion-based* gifting (anniversaries, bonuses). We do *onboarding-specific* swag. These actually complement each other."

**Use-Case Segmentation Counter:**
"Here's how it breaks down:
- **SwagUp/Bonusly:** Employee recognition, milestone gifts, ad-hoc fun stuff
- **Our tool:** Onboarding kits, team events, new-role gifting

Most teams realize they need both because the use cases are different. Onboarding is fast-moving and brand-heavy. Recognition gifting is slower and more casual."

**Low-Friction Integration:**
"And here's the good news: You don't rip out your current tool. You add us for the onboarding flow. Two tools, zero disruption."

**Pilot Approach:**
"Best way to test: Use us for your next onboarding cohort (2-3 weeks). Keep everything else the same. If it works, we integrate. If not, you're out $4,800 and back to your current setup."

**Check Understanding:**
"Does using us *alongside* your current vendor, not instead of, make this more doable?"

### OBJECTION #4: "10 minutes to a live store sounds impossible. Prove it."
**Risk Level:** 🔴 HIGH (Skepticism about core claim)  
**Why it comes up:** People Ops folks have been burned by "fast" vendors before.

**Listen For:**
- "I'm skeptical something that good is real."
- "We've heard claims before that didn't pan out."
- "What's the catch?"

**Validation:**
"Healthy skepticism. And I get why—you've probably heard 'we're fast' before and it wasn't."

**Proof in the Call:**
"Let me show you. I'll enter [their domain], and you time me. Watch the brand extraction run, then the mockup generate."

[Type domain → hit "Extract" → watch brand data pull → show mockup preview → check clock]

"3 minutes. The remaining 7 minutes is procurement/order placement, which is on you. But from 'domain' to 'live storefront with products' is around 10 total."

**Why It's Fast:**
"Here's the secret: We're not doing custom design. We're:
1. Extracting brand auto-magically (Brandfetch API)
2. Generating mockups from your brand (template + colors)
3. Pushing to Shopify (API integration)

No designers, no back-and-forth, no approval cycles. That's why it's fast."

**Social Proof:**
"We've timed this with [peer company] and [peer company]. Fastest was 6 minutes. Slowest was 14. Average is 9."

**Check Understanding:**
"Does seeing the 3-minute extraction + mockup help prove the timeline?"

### OBJECTION #5: "$24K feels steep for a 6-month pilot. Can we start smaller?"
**Risk Level:** ⚠️ MEDIUM (Price negotiation)  
**Why it comes up:** Budget gating. Finance wants smaller commitment.

**Listen For:**
- "Can we do $12K instead?"
- "What's the minimum to get started?"
- "We need to prove ROI before bigger spending."

**Validation:**
"Fair ask. And I get why you want to test before committing."

**Reframe—Tiers Explained:**
"Here's how the pricing works:
- **$12K** = 500 credits/month = ~2-3 small orders or 1 medium order
- **$24K** = 1,000 credits/month = 4-5 orders or 1 large order
- **$36K** = 1,500 credits/month = unlimited flexibility

The jump from $12K to $24K isn't arbitrary. It's the difference between 'testing with one cohort' and 'running two cohorts or scaling.' Most teams find $12K too constrained. You hit the limit in month 2 and can't iterate."

**Pilot Path (Lower Entry Point):**
"Here's what I actually recommend: Do the **$4,800 Brand Drop Pilot** first. That's one onboarding kit, full feedback, no commitment. Proves the value. Then we talk $12K or $24K based on your demand."

"I'd rather have you start at $4.8K and move to $24K than commit $12K and be frustrated by the constraints."

**ROI Calculation:**
"Let's say 50 new hires per quarter, $100 per kit = $5K spend. Improving delight NPS by 30% = X% better retention = $Y annual value. The $24K investment pays for itself in better onboarding experience alone."

**Check Understanding:**
"Does starting with the $4,800 pilot to prove ROI, then moving to a full tier, feel like the right path?"

## Objection-by-Persona

### Chief People Officer (CPO) / VP People Ops
**Primary Concern:** "Will this actually improve employee experience?"

**Objection Playbook:**
1. Lead with **onboarding outcomes** (NPS, retention signal)
2. Emphasize **employee choice** (self-selection delight)
3. Anchor to **brand consistency** (CPO usually owns brand guardianship)
4. Offer **30-day feedback cycle** (quick learning loop)

**Key Counter:**
"Your job is making onboarding memorable. Swag that *employees choose* lands better than swag that *you choose*. That's our differentiator."

---

### Manager / People Team IC
**Primary Concern:** "Will this make my job easier or harder?"

**Objection Playbook:**
1. Lead with **time savings** (10-minute onboarding flow)
2. Emphasize **fewer approvals** (brand auto-extracted, mockup approved)
3. Highlight **self-service fulfillment** (they don't have to manage production)
4. Offer **API integration** (connects to HRIS, no manual work)

**Key Counter:**
"We're not adding work. We're automating the boring part (design + vendor coordination) so you can focus on employee experience."

---

### Finance / Procurement
**Primary Concern:** "Is this ROI-positive?"

**Objection Playbook:**
1. Lead with **cost replacement** (not new spend)
2. Emphasize **retention lift** ($X value per 1% retention gain)
3. Highlight **unit economics** (cost per employee improved)
4. Offer **pilot model** (small commitment, proof before scale)

**Key Counter:**
"Your current swag spend is baked in. We're helping you get more value from the same spend through better targeting and employee experience. The ROI is in retention, not discount."

## Handling "No Thank You" Gracefully

If prospect decides not to pilot:

**Script:**
"I totally understand. Not every tool is the right fit for every team. Two asks before we wrap:

1. **Who should we talk to?** If you know another team lead (People Ops or Talent) who might find this valuable, I'd love an intro.

2. **What would have to change?** If you were going to explore this again in 6 months, what needs to shift? Budget? Timing? Feature X?"

**Why it works:**
- Preserves relationship for future warm path
- Gets valuable feedback on product/positioning
- Offers peer intro (improves overall warm-path conversion)

## Victory Conditions

### Best-Case Response
"This looks great. Let's do the $4,800 Brand Drop Pilot starting [date]. I'll need [your domain, one sample onboarding cohort size]. Send over an SOW?"

**Next Steps:** Send SOW within 24h, set start date, begin order process

### Good-Case Response
"I want to talk to my [manager / finance / CMO] and get back to you in [72h / week]. Can we sync again [day]?"

**Next Steps:** Confirm follow-up call, send summary email with key talking points, follow up 48h before scheduled call

### Moderate-Case Response
"The tool is cool, but timing isn't right. What about [3 months / after new hire wave]?"

**Next Steps:** Log in CRM with future follow-up date, add to nurture drip, stay in touch

### Poor-Case Response
"Not a fit right now."

**Next Steps:** Gracefully exit, ask for peer intro, document objection, offer to re-engage in 6 months

## Post-Call Debrief Questions

After each call, ask yourself:

1. **Which assumption(s) did this call validate most?** A1, A2, A3, A4, A5, or A6?
2. **What surprised me about their response?**
3. **What objection came up that we should counter better?**
4. **Did they reference any specific pain point we could address in positioning?**
5. **What's their next decision step and timeline?**

---

## Continuous Improvement

### After 2-3 Calls
Review which objections came up most frequently. Refine counters based on what landed.

### After 5 Calls
Synthesize top 3 objections + best-performing counters. Build objection library for wider sales team.

### Feedback Loop
Each call informs objection playbook for next call. Over time, you'll see patterns and develop "anti-patterns" (what *doesn't* work).
