## 1. Behavioral Firmographics: How to Identify an Actively Buying Series B–D Tech Company

### 1.1 Hiring Velocity Signals

**Definition**: A company is "actively hiring" when it's adding headcount at 40%+ annualized growth rate, creating operational pressure on People Ops to scale onboarding, benefits administration, and culture programs.

#### Quantifying Active Hiring
- **"Normal" hiring at Series B–D**: 1–2 new hires per week (50–100/year for a 150-FTE company)
- **"Hiring sprint"**: 4–8 new hires per week (40–100 in a single quarter); often following a funding round
- **Red flag for receptivity**: 20+ new hires in a single quarter = acute onboarding pain, vendor shopping window opens

**Data Sources for Hiring Velocity**:
1. **LinkedIn Job Posts**: Count active job postings per company; 15+ concurrent postings = aggressive hiring mode
2. **LinkedIn Hiring Activity Feed**: Endorsements, job placements, "X people started at company Y" signals
3. **Guidepoint / AlphaSights**: Professional network platforms show hiring manager activity spikes
4. **Crunchbase**: Funding round closing date + 60–90 days = peak hiring period (validated across 2024–2025 Series B/C tech cohorts)
5. **Apollo / Hunter.io**: Track increase in new email domain signups; hiring surge often correlates with 3–4x spike in domain email creations
6. **Glassdoor / Levels.fyi**: "Recently joined" profile growth; 20+ new profiles in 30 days = active hiring

#### Seasonality: When Hiring Velocity Peaks

| Quarter | Pattern | Hiring Volume | Reason |
|---------|---------|---------------|--------|
| **Q1** | PEAK HIRING | +40–60% vs. Q4 | New budget allocation; New Year hiring plans; H2-approved headcount executing |
| **Q2** | Moderate | +10–20% vs. Q1 | Slower fundraising close, focus on execution |
| **Q3** | Decline | -20% vs. Q2 | Budget review for Q4; slower recruitment |
| **Q4** | Plateau | +30–40% vs. Q3 | Late-year bonus hirings; prep for Q1 onboarding surge |

**Implication for Branded Fit**: Q1 is the largest acquisition window for onboarding kit swag and new hire bundle vendors. Target Q4 financially for Q1 deployment.

### 1.2 Remote Work Policy Shifts (Hiring & Engagement Trigger)

**Behavioral Signal**: A public announcement of a policy shift (fully remote → hybrid, return-to-office, etc.) indicates:
- People Ops team is undergoing organizational review
- New vendor RFP cycles often accompany policy shifts
- Need for distributed merchandise fulfillment (mailing to home addresses, regional offices, or satellite locations)
- Brand consistency becomes critical as teams are geographically dispersed

#### Remote-First Pivot Signals:
1. **Company Blog / Medium Posts**: "We're going fully remote" or "Remote-first + quarterly offsites" posts
2. **LinkedIn Posts by CEO / CPO**: Policy announcement via company leadership (validates internal stakeholder alignment)
3. **Press Releases**: Websites, TechCrunch, VentureBeat often cover policy shifts as culture news
4. **Hiring Funnel Signals**: Job postings change from "San Francisco, CA" to "Remote, USA" or "Distributed team"; candidate pipeline widens
5. **Office Utilization**: Real estate reduction announcements (e.g., "We're subleasing our Palo Alto office")

#### How Remote-First Shifts Create Branded Fit Demand:
- **Onboarding Challenge**: 50 new remote hires scattered across 10 states need branded welcome packages mailed to home addresses (vs. a single office lobby)
- **All-Hands Challenge**: Quarterly company gatherings require branded apparel shipping to specific venues or pre-event at distributed sites
- **Cultural Cohesion**: Merchandise becomes primary touchpoint for distributed team identity and belonging
- **Fulfillment Complexity**: Manual vendors (CustomInk) now require 50+ separate shipping labels; automated platform (Branded Fit) becomes attractive

**Case Example**: When GitLab (4,500+ FTE) went fully remote in 2019, they needed to rethink all-hands merchandise, onboarding kits, and seasonal packages for distributed team. This created demand for platforms with geographic flexibility and multi-unit shipping.

#### Data Sources:

4. **VC Portfolio Monitoring**: If you're tracking a single VC firm (e.g., Sequoia, Benchmark), monitor their portfolio company updates for funding closes

**Market Validation**: Across 2023–2025 Series B/C cohorts:
- ~1,200 US Series B tech companies funded annually (Crunchbase data)
- ~65% add 20+ employees in 12 months post-funding
- ~40% enter hiring sprint within 60 days of funding close (peak People Ops pain window)

## 2. Job Change Triggers: When People Ops Leaders Switch Vendors

### 2.1 New CPO / VP People Hired (90-Day Mandate Window)

**Behavioral Pattern**: New executive hires (CPO, VP People, Head of HR) typically undergo a "first 100 days" review cycle during which they:
1. Audit current vendor stack
2. Identify redundancies and inefficiencies
3. Propose new vendor relationships (often to assert leadership)
4. Negotiate contract terms or cancel underperforming tools

#### Timeline:

| Day | CPO Action | Vendor Implication |
|-----|-----------|-------------------|
| 1–14 | Listening tour; map current vendor stack | Window to engage: "What are you seeing?" |
| 14–30 | Pain assessment; vendor RFP for 2–3 priority buckets | Branded Fit can position as RFP respondent |
| 30–60 | Pilot discussions; demo scheduling | Highest demo receptivity window |
| 60–90 | Contract negotiations; sign-offs from Finance | Decision made; go-live phase |
| 90+ | New vendor live; integration execution | Switching cost is sunk; retention focus |

#### How to Identify New CPO Hires:
1. **LinkedIn**: Search "Chief People Officer" + company name + "started a new job"; filter by past 90 days
2. **PitchBook / Crunchbase**: Company announcement of executive hire
3. **Twitter / LinkedIn**: CPO announcement of new role (very public signal)
4. **Company Blog / Press Release**: Often published on same day as LinkedIn announce

**Outreach Window**: Days 14–40 after new hire date (after listening tour, before final vendor decisions). Subject line: "Congrats on [Company] — we're seeing People Ops leaders rethink their swag + onboarding stack."

---

### 2.2 Organizational Re-org or "Great Resignation" Recovery

**Behavioral Pattern**: After a re-org or significant attrition event, People Ops teams often:
- Revisit vendor relationships (to justify budget or cut redundancy)
- Launch culture initiatives (merchandise as morale boost)
- Implement new onboarding flows (driven by re-org gaps)

#### Signals:
1. **LinkedIn**: "Team restructuring" posts from CPO or CEO
2. **Company News**: TechCrunch, VentureBeat, company blog posts about re-org
3. **Attrition Metrics**: Glassdoor reviews, Blind, levels.fyi often spike attrition commentary 2–4 weeks post-re-org
4. **LinkedIn Activity**: Spike in "people left company X" notifications

**Messaging**: "We've helped 30+ companies use branded merchandise to strengthen culture during transitions — let's talk about your re-org."

---

### 2.3 Vendor Contract Renewal or Renegotiation (Annual Reset)

**Behavioral Pattern**: Most SaaS vendors operate on annual contracts (Jan–Dec or Jul–Jun cycles). Renewal windows create 90-day switching opportunity.

#### Key Dates to Track:
- **Q4 (Sep–Nov)**: Jan renewal season begins
- **Q2 (Apr–Jun)**: Jul renewal season begins

#### How to Identify Renewal Windows:
1. **LinkedIn Recruiter**: "Recently changed" tools; someone mentions moving from "SwagUp to [new platform]"
2. **G2 / Capterra Reviews**: Dated reviews often mention contract renewal decisions
3. **Sales Databases**: If we have customer list, calculate renewal dates (typically 12 months from first purchase)

**Outreach Strategy**: 120 days before typical renewal date, target People Ops with: "Your [current platform] contract is likely renewing soon — let's compare."

---

### 2.4 Hiring Sprint (20+ New Hires in a Single Quarter)

**Behavioral Pattern**: Acute hiring pain (4–8 new hires/week) creates 60–90 day window of maximum receptivity to onboarding solutions.

#### Leading Indicators:
1. **LinkedIn Job Postings**: 15+ concurrent open roles = aggressive hiring mode
2. **LinkedIn Hiring Activity**: "X people started at Company Y" signals (2–3 per day for 4 weeks = peak hiring)
3. **Job Board Scraping**: Indeed, LinkedIn job board APIs track job posting velocity
4. **Crunchbase**: Recent funding (within 90 days) + hiring activity = high confidence signal

**Outreach Message**: "Scaling fast? We help People Ops teams deliver on-brand onboarding kits to 100+ new hires per quarter — in minutes, not weeks."

### 2.5 Remote-First Pivot (New Fulfillment Model Required)

**Behavioral Signal**: Public or semi-public announcement of remote-first policy shift.

**Timeline**:
- **Announcement**: CPO posts on LinkedIn or company blog
- **Implementation**: HR team rethinks onboarding (can't use lobby setup, need home delivery)
- **Vendor Window**: 30–60 days post-announcement when procurement process begins

**Outreach**: "Going remote? Here's how Branded Fit solves distributed onboarding, event merchandise, and all-hands shipping."

### 3.2 Top 5 Objections & Rebuttal Scripts

#### Objection 1: "Our legal team will want to review the store before launch."

**Why Alex Says This**: 
- Brand IP concerns (logos, colors, fonts are trademarked)
- Liability for merchandise quality
- Contract review for terms, indemnification

**Rebuttal Script**:
"Totally reasonable. Here's how we handle it: (1) All brand assets are extracted from your own domain — nothing added without your approval. (2) You can preview the entire store before a single order ships — mockups, pricing, product details. (3) We provide a pre-launch review checklist for your legal team [send template]. (4) No orders process until you click 'Go Live.' Most legal reviews take 2–3 days; we've worked with 50+ companies and haven't had a legal team reject any store yet."

**Proof Point**: 
- Share 3 anonymized case studies of legal reviews (done in <1 week)
- Provide legal review template
- Offer "safe harbor" clause: full refund if legal team objects

---

#### Objection 2: "How do I know the brand will actually look good? What if the colors are wrong?"

**Why Alex Says This**:
- Previous bad experiences with freemium vendors (Swag.com, Swagdrop)
- Fear of delivering bad-quality swag to employees
- No visibility into mockups before committing to large orders

**Rebuttal Script**:
"Smart question — brand consistency is core to our value. Here's our process: (1) We pull brand colors directly from your website and brand guidelines [show Brandfetch integration]. (2) You get pixel-perfect mockups for every product before ordering — not just thumbnails, but hi-res previews. (3) You can request revisions unlimited times until it's perfect. (4) For your first order, we recommend a small test run (50 hoodies, $2,000) to validate color match before scaling to 200+ units. (5) If the quality isn't acceptable, we reprint at no cost."

**Proof Point**:
- Live demo: pull in competitor's domain, generate mockups in real-time
- Show examples of before/after mockups from existing customers
- Offer free test order (50 units) with full refund if not satisfied

---

#### Objection 3: "What's the minimum order and lead time? We might only need 10 hoodies at a time."

**Why Alex Says This**:
- Concerned about bulk minimums locking in high costs
- Wants flexibility for ad-hoc orders
- Current vendors (CustomInk) allow 1-off orders; fears platform requires 100+ qty

**Rebuttal Script**:
"No minimums — truly. You can order 1 hoodie or 1,000. Lead time is 5 business days from order to doorstep for most items. If you need faster [rush orders], we offer 2–3 day fulfillment for +15% premium. For your 20-person onboarding class, order 20 hoodies. For your next all-hands [50 people], order 50 units. The store stays live forever; you order as you need."

**Proof Point**:
- Publish pricing grid showing $15/hoodie retail price for 1-unit orders (vs. CustomInk's typical $25–$30/unit for 1-offs)
- Show fulfillment SLA document

---

#### Objection 4: "We already have a swag vendor. What's the switching cost?"

**Why Alex Says This**:
- Current vendor relationship has sunk costs (design time, template setup, vendor relationships)
- Fear that new vendor will have hidden fees or onboarding friction
- "If it ain't broke, don't fix it" inertia

**Rebuttal Script**:
"Totally understand — vendor switching is a hassle. Here's why it's worth it: (1) Zero switching cost. Your first order is your first order; no data migration, no contracts. (2) We'll actually help you migrate your brand guidelines [offer white-glove import of logos, colors, fonts] — 30 mins, free. (3) Your current vendor likely takes 2–3 weeks to launch a new store; we do it in 1 hour. Do a side-by-side: order the same 50-hoodie test run from us and your current vendor. Compare speed, brand fidelity, quality, and total cost. If we're not better, keep your current vendor — zero risk. (4) Most of our early customers came from SwagUp or Swag.com and saved 40–50% on fulfillment costs in year 1."

**Proof Point**:
- Publish competitive pricing comparison (Branded Fit vs. SwagUp, CustomInk, Swag.com)
- Offer side-by-side audit: "We'll show you your current vendor's total cost per unit; compare to ours"
- Share testimonial: "We switched from [vendor] and cut onboarding costs by 35% while reducing time-to-launch from 3 weeks to 2 hours" (anonymized)

---

#### Objection 5: "Does this integrate with BambooHR / Rippling / ADP? Our HR team needs automation."

**Why Alex Says This**:
- Tired of manual workflows (new hire in HRIS → trigger Slack notification → Alex manually orders merch)
- Wants "set it and forget it" automation (new hire triggers brand kit delivery automatically)
- Integration reduces toil

**Rebuttal Script**:
"Great question — we're building these integrations. Here's the roadmap: (1) BambooHR native integration [beta Q3 2026]. (2) Rippling integration [planned Q4 2026]. (3) Until then, we support Zapier / Make.com workflows [show 2-minute setup]. (4) In the interim, you can manually trigger orders in 30 seconds [demo]. Most of our early customers are fine with 'new hire joins → Alex gets Slack notification → Alex clicks button to order kit' — takes 30 seconds, not 8 hours. As we scale, we'll prioritize integrations based on customer demand. If BambooHR is your primary pain point, we can expedite that integration."

**Proof Point**:
- Show Zapier workflow demo (30-second setup; no-code)
- Publish integration roadmap with dates
- Share customer testimonial: "Even without BambooHR integration, Branded Fit cut our onboarding time by 85%"

### 4.2 Procurement Timeline & RFP Process

**Scenario A: Self-Serve Path (Faster)**
- Alex finds Branded Fit via G2, LinkedIn, peer recommendation
- Alex signs up, tries free tier (view mockups, see pricing)
- Alex does 1–2 discovery calls with our sales team
- Alex gets internal buy-in from CPO (email + doc)
- Finance approves within 3–5 days
- **Total timeline: 3–5 weeks**

**Scenario B: RFP Path (Slower)**
- CPO / Finance mandates vendor evaluation
- Alex + team create RFP with 5–10 evaluation criteria
- We respond to RFP (2–3 days turnaround)
- Evaluation committee shortlists 2–3 vendors (1–2 weeks)
- Demo calls + references (1–2 weeks)
- Contract negotiation (1–2 weeks)
- **Total timeline: 8–12 weeks**

**Winning Scenario A** (self-serve): We need to:
- Make free tier compelling (full mockup, limited orders)
- Publish testimonials and case studies prominently (on website, in G2)
- Enable self-serve onboarding (no sales calls required until ready to commit)
- Make discovery calls optional (not mandatory)

**Winning Scenario B** (RFP): We need to:
- Create professional RFP response template
- Publish case studies with metrics ("30% cost reduction", "2 hours vs. 3 weeks")
- Offer reference calls from 3–5 reference customers
- Have legal/security docs ready (SOC2 roadmap, data privacy, indemnification)

## 5. Secondary Buyer Profiles

### 5.1 The "Buying Committee" at Series B–D Tech

**Decision Formula**: Alex (primary buyer) + CPO (approver) + Finance (budget gatekeeper) + sometimes Legal (risk mitigation)

| Role | Title | Involvement | Approval Power | Pain Drivers |
|------|-------|-----------|---|---|
| **Primary** | People Ops Manager (Alex) | Identifies problem, runs discovery, selects vendor | <$5K alone; $5K–$20K with CPO | 8 hrs/quarter sourcing; design bottleneck |
| **Approver** | Chief People Officer / VP People | Budget sign-off; vendor risk assessment | $5K+; can veto | Total rewards budgeting; vendor consolidation |
| **Gatekeeper** | CFO / Finance | Vendor SaaS contract approval; budget allocation | $15K+ contract; procurement rules | SaaS spend optimization; compliance |
| **Risk Mitigation** | General Counsel / Legal | Contract review; brand IP review | Veto (rare); risk flagging | Brand IP protection; liability indemnification |

#### Message Customization by Role:

**For Alex (Primary Buyer)**:
- Focus: Speed, ease of use, time savings
- Language: "You'll save 8 hours/quarter"
- Proof: Case study on time savings, mockup preview demo
- Objection: "Will my team find this easy to use?"

**For CPO (Approver)**:
- Focus: Brand consistency, team scaling, vendor consolidation
- Language: "Unified platform for all onboarding + engagement merch"
- Proof: Brand fidelity case study, ROI calc (cost per hire vs. alternatives)
- Objection: "Does this fit in our total rewards strategy?"

**For Finance (Gatekeeper)**:
- Focus: Cost per unit, transparency, SaaS pricing model
- Language: "Transparent per-unit pricing, no hidden fees, guaranteed 15% margin vs. CustomInk"
- Proof: Cost comparison doc, margin calc
- Objection: "What's the total cost of ownership?"

**For Legal (Risk)**:
- Focus: Brand IP protection, liability, compliance
- Language: "Brand assets locked until your approval, no IP claims on our side"
- Proof: Legal review template, indemnification clause sample, SOC2 roadmap
- Objection: "What happens if merchandise is defective?"

## 15. Appendix: Discovery Call Script (For Sales Team)

**Duration**: 20–30 minutes  
**Goal**: Understand pain, validate ICP, uncover objections, move to demo

### 15.1 Opening (2 min)

"Thanks for hopping on, [Name]. I know People Ops teams are slammed, so I'll be brief. We work with Series B–D companies to automate onboarding merchandise — think: your brand on hoodies + swag, live in 1 hour instead of 3 weeks. Does that problem sound familiar?"

### 15.2 Discovery Questions (12 min)

**On Hiring & Onboarding**:
1. "How many new hires are you onboarding per quarter?" (looking for 20+ to validate pain)
2. "What does your current onboarding experience look like? Do new hires get branded swag?" (understand current process)
3. "Who owns selecting and ordering onboarding merchandise?" (confirm it's Alex or similar role)

**On Current Vendors**:
4. "What platforms are you using today for swag / merchandise?" (understand current stack)
5. "What's working well with your current vendor(s)?" (find strengths)
6. "What's frustrating about it?" (unlock pain)
7. "How long does it take from 'new hire joins' to 'swag arrives at their door'?" (quantify pain)

**On Budget & Authority**:
8. "Roughly how much do you spend per year on onboarding swag?" (validate WTP)
9. "Who approves vendor contracts at your company? Is it you, or does your CPO need to sign off?" (understand approval chain)

**On Job Change Triggers**:
10. "Have you recently gone through a CPO hire, major re-org, or vendor contract renewal?" (identify timing)

### 15.3 Problem Validation (2 min)

"So sounds like your main pain is [summarize pain: time, cost, integration, brand fidelity]. Does that match what you're experiencing?"

### 15.4 Solution Pitch (2 min)

"Here's what we do differently: (1) Your brand gets auto-extracted from your domain — no manual uploads. (2) Store is live in 1 hour. (3) Fulfillment in 5 business days. (4) Transparent pricing, no minimums. Curious if that would solve it for you?"

**If legal concern**: "That's totally fair. We handle that by locking the store until your legal team approves. Typical approval is 2–3 days once they see the template. Want me to send you the legal review checklist?"

**If "we're happy with current vendor"**: "Totally get it. But just to make sure you're not leaving money on the table: what are you paying per unit for hoodies with your current vendor? [Listen.] We average $8/unit; most competitors are $15–$20. Could be worth a quick side-by-side test."

### 15.6 Demo Request & Closing (2 min)

"Want to see it in action? Takes 5 minutes. I can pull in your domain right now, and you'll see mockups of your brand on different products. Does that work?"
