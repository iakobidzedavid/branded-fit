# DE Step 12 — Determine the Decision-Making Unit (DMU)
**Branded Fit · Beachhead: US Series A/B SaaS, 75–300 FTEs · Date: 2026-06-19**

---

## Executive context

Branded Fit sells a $2,400/yr subscription + per-order economics to a 120-FTE Series A/B SaaS company. The product (domain → branded Shopify storefront in 10 minutes) screams self-serve, but the purchase ticket — an annual platform fee plus a first swag order typically running $3,000–$8,000 — crosses the approval threshold of most People Ops generalists. Understanding *who* is inside the buying decision, what each person cares about, and how they interact with each other is the difference between a 14-day sales cycle and a 90-day stall.

This step maps every human role that touches the purchase of Branded Fit at a representative beachhead account. The DMU is not a list of job titles — it is a causal network: who initiates, who investigates, who advocates internally, who can veto, who signs, and who receives value. Getting this wrong means we write cold emails to people who can't buy, try to demo to people who can't care, and structure the product around the wrong "wow" moment.

The foundation is Steps 3, 6, 9, and 13 (which partially reconstructed the DMU when Step 12 previously failed). This document extends that reconstruction into a complete, operationally useful DMU map with messaging, objections, and activation plays for each role.

---

## 1. The Buying Context

### Why the DMU is more complex than PLG implies

Branded Fit's activation flow is PLG-shaped: Maya discovers the product on Slack, pastes her domain, and has a live storefront preview in under 10 minutes. If the ACV were $500/yr, Maya signs with her personal AmEx and the DMU is one person. But the economics of the beachhead make that impossible:

- **Platform fee:** $2,400/yr (or $199/mo). Above Maya's unilateral spend floor at most firms.
- **First swag order:** typically $3,000–$8,000. Requires Finance approval and possibly a vendor-onboarding form at 150+ FTE firms.
- **Employee PII (shipping addresses):** any tool that stores home addresses for gift shipments triggers an IT/InfoSec review at security-conscious Series B companies.
- **Brand asset exposure:** the AI uses the company's logo and color palette. A Head of Brand or Marketing will want to see the output before it goes live.

The result is a **PLG-assisted hybrid motion**: one person discovers and falls in love with the product, but 2–4 people must not actively object (or must actively co-sign) for money to move. Mapping the full DMU is the prerequisite to engineering a sales motion that accounts for all of them.

### The 6 DMU roles at a Series A/B SaaS (75–300 FTE)

| Role | DMU Label | Typical Title | Appears at Headcount |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maya — the operator | **Champion / End User** | People Ops Manager / People Ops Generalist | 75–300 FTE (always present) |
| Her boss | **Economic Buyer / Approver** | Head of People / VP People | 100+ FTE; COO at <100 FTE |
| Finance | **Purchaser / Budget Gatekeeper** | Controller / Head of Finance / CFO | 100+ FTE; Founder at <100 FTE |
| Brand/Marketing | **Technical Influencer** | Head of Brand / Brand Designer / VP Marketing | 150+ FTE; absent below |
| IT/InfoSec | **Technical Vetter** | Head of IT / IT Manager / InfoSec Lead | 200+ FTE; ad-hoc below |
| Incumbent vendor | **Saboteur** | Account Manager at SwagUp, Stadium, or agency | Present when contract >$5K/yr |

## 2. Individual DMU Role Profiles

### Role 1 — Champion / End User: Maya Chen (People Ops Manager)

**Who she is.** 28–34 years old, solo or +1 coordinator, responsible for all of HR ops: onboarding, offboarding, HRIS administration, swag, culture programs, and remote-team logistics. Per Step 3, she burns 6–12 hours per swag cycle on a toolchain of Printify, Canva, Notion, a 3PL, and Slack. Branded Fit is the first product she has ever seen that collapses that to a single flow.

**What she cares about (in order):**
1. Time reclaimed — anything that cuts her ops overhead so her performance review reflects culture outcomes, not logistics.
2. Brand output quality — she will not ship something ugly; her team will blame her if swag looks off-brand.
3. Defensibility with her manager — she needs to walk into her Head of People's Slack with a 2-sentence justification, not a business case.
4. Employee delight — redemption rate is her proxy metric; she tracks who picked up their hoodie.

**Her typical objection:** "I love it, but I need to check with [Head of People / Finance] before I can commit."

**Her activation moment (the "wow"):** The first time she pastes her company domain and sees a full-color, logo-correct storefront preview with 10 products in under 5 minutes. This is the moment she becomes an internal advocate.

**How she finds us:**
- Organic: peer Slack DM in `#peopleops`, `#remote-onboarding`, or `#swag` channels (People Geeks, Modern Employer, Lattice RFH community).
- Paid: LinkedIn carousel ad targeting "People Ops Manager, Series A/B SaaS."
- Cold outbound: personalized email with a pre-generated storefront preview for her specific domain.

**Message that lands:** *"You told us swag eats 8 hours of your week. Here's what your company's store looks like — took us 4 minutes. No design review needed."*

**Sales play:** Close Maya first on enthusiasm, then arm her with the internal sell kit (1-page PDF + ROI calculator). Her job is to tee up the Head of People meeting, not to close independently.

### Role 2 — Economic Buyer / Approver: Head of People / VP People

**Who they are.** Maya's direct manager. At a 120-FTE Series A/B, this is frequently a Director-level or VP-level individual hired 6–18 months ago to professionalize HR as the company scaled from 50 to 150. They manage Maya plus 1–2 others (recruiter, HR coordinator), report to the CEO or COO, and sit in the ELT. They care about retention, employer brand, and People-team efficiency — not about swag product UX.

**What they care about (in order):**
1. Retention and culture ROI — swag is a line item they defend to the CFO as "culture infrastructure."
2. Their team's capacity — if Maya is burning 8 hrs/week on swag and Branded Fit reclaims it, that's real leverage for a Head of People with a thin team.
3. Brand safety — they do not want to be the reason a bad-looking hoodie shipped to 80 new hires.
4. Vendor simplicity — they are already managing 20+ SaaS contracts; a new vendor must justify its overhead.

**Their typical objection:** "What's the annual cost all-in, including orders? And does Finance need to onboard this as a new vendor?" They are cost-conscious and process-aware, not technophobic.

**Their activation moment:** Seeing Maya's 5-minute demo plus the ROI calculation (44 hours/yr reclaimed at Maya's fully-loaded rate ≈ $5,500/yr value vs. $2,400/yr fee). They flip from skeptic to ally when the math is obvious and Maya is visibly excited.

**How we reach them:**
- Through Maya — she is the internal champion. We should NOT cold-reach the Head of People before Maya has had her own wow moment.
- If cold outbound is required (e.g., no Maya-titled contact visible): LinkedIn + email using the Head of People's retention/culture frame ("save your team 44 hours a year on swag ops").

**Message that lands:** *"Your People Ops generalist spent 8 hours last quarter coordinating the holiday kit. Branded Fit gives her that back — and the swag looks better. Here's what your store would look like today."*

**Sales play:** A 20-minute async demo video (Loom) pre-built for their specific company, sent by Maya, is more persuasive than a vendor-scheduled demo call. Arm Maya with this artifact.

---

### Role 3 — Purchaser / Budget Gatekeeper: Controller / Head of Finance

**Who they are.** At a 120-FTE Series A/B, the Finance gatekeeper is typically a Controller or Head of Finance (not yet a CFO). They receive a vendor-onboarding request from Maya or the Head of People, check whether there is budget in the People Ops line, and approve the PO. They are not a product evaluator — they are a budget and compliance gate.

**What they care about (in order):**
1. Budget availability — is there a line item in the People budget for this? If not, can it be coded to culture/onboarding?
2. Annual contract clarity — they strongly prefer an annual PO over a month-to-month subscription because it simplifies accruals.
3. Vendor legitimacy — does Branded Fit have a W-9, a standard MSA, and a privacy policy that covers employee PII?
4. No surprise overage — the per-order economics must be clearly documented so there are no surprise invoices.

**Their typical objection:** "Is there budget for this? Do we have an existing vendor we're replacing?"

**Their activation moment:** There is no "wow" for Finance — there is only an unblocked gate. The goal is to make their checklist frictionless: pre-prepared W-9, clear annual fee + per-order pricing sheet, standard MSA with a data-processing addendum (DPA) for employee shipping addresses, and a named budget line in the People Ops cost center.

**How we reach them:** We do not cold-reach Finance. They receive a vendor onboarding packet from Maya or the Head of People. Our job is to make that packet so complete that Finance's review takes 15 minutes.

**Message that lands (in the vendor packet):** *"Branded Fit charges $2,400/yr (flat) plus a 15% platform margin on each swag order, invoiced per order. No minimums, no overages. W-9, MSA, and DPA attached. Replaces SwagUp/Printify line items in the People budget."*

**Sales play:** Create a one-page "Finance FAQ" document as a downloadable artifact in the product — Maya downloads it and attaches it to her vendor-request email to Finance.

---

### Role 4 — Technical Influencer: Head of Brand / Brand Designer

**Who they are.** At 150+ FTE Series A/B companies, there is often a brand designer or small in-house creative team. This person cares deeply about brand consistency and will view any AI-generated brand output with suspicion. At <150 FTE, this role is typically absent or collapsed into the Head of Marketing, who rarely involves themselves in swag unless it's for a major launch.

**What they care about:**
1. Brand fidelity — does the AI correctly apply the logo, hex colors, and font system?
2. Product aesthetics — is the mockup quality credible? Will a pilled Gildan tee show up with their premium logo on it?
3. Control — they want the ability to adjust the storefront before it goes live. Maya assuring them "it looks right" is not enough.

**Their typical objection:** "I need to see the actual output before anything goes live. The AI might apply the colors wrong."

**Their activation moment:** A side-by-side of the Brandfetch-extracted brand assets vs. the generated storefront, with an edit-before-launch interface. If the Brand Designer can tweak a hex value and see it update in real time, they shift from veto to co-champion.

**How we reach them:** Only through Maya. We should not cold-reach Brand. Tactically: the storefront preview email Maya shares should include a "Preview & Edit" link that requires no login — so the Brand Designer can review and approve without creating an account.

**Message that lands:** *"Maya invited you to review the brand output before anything is published. You can edit colors, swap products, and adjust the layout. Nothing goes live until you approve."*

**Sales play:** Build a no-login "Brand Review" shareable link into the product. This turns the Brand Designer from a blocker into a stakeholder who feels respected, and removes Maya's need to negotiate approval manually.

---

### Role 5 — Technical Vetter: IT / InfoSec Lead

**Who they are.** At 200+ FTE Series A/B companies (especially fintech, dev tools, or security-category SaaS), any new vendor that stores employee data triggers a security review. This is typically a Head of IT, IT Manager, or Security Engineer. Their bar is a completed vendor security questionnaire (VSQ), a SOC 2 report or equivalent, and a data processing agreement (DPA) covering GDPR/CCPA if applicable.

**What they care about:**
1. Data residency and access controls — where are employee shipping addresses stored? Who can access them?
2. Vendor security posture — does Branded Fit have a VSQ on file? SOC 2 Type II (or at least Type I)?
3. Integration security — does connecting via Shopify OAuth create a risk surface?

**Their typical objection:** "We need to complete a vendor security assessment before we can approve this." This can add 7–21 days to the close cycle.

**Their activation moment:** A pre-filled VSQ and a clear data-flow diagram (employee PII → Printify for shipping → deleted after order fulfillment) removes the major risk surface. If Branded Fit does not store employee addresses beyond order fulfillment, that is a significant selling point.

**How we reach them:** Only through Maya or the Head of People (who forwards a vendor-onboarding intake). Pre-empt the review by including a security one-pager in the vendor packet.

**Message that lands:** *"Employee addresses are passed directly to Printify at order time and are not retained on Branded Fit servers beyond 30-day order history. A completed VSQ and our privacy policy are attached."*

**Sales play:** Publish a public Trust Center page (trust.brandedfit.com equivalent) with: completed VSQ template, data flow diagram, retention policy, and contact for security reviews. Maya links to this instead of waiting for a custom response.

---

### Role 6 — Saboteur: Incumbent Vendor Account Manager

**Who they are.** At any beachhead account currently spending $5K+/yr with SwagUp, Stadium, a Printfection contract, or a swag agency (BAMKO, Geiger, etc.), there is an incumbent vendor AM who will notice when renewal discussions stall. Their playbook: remind Maya of switching costs ("you'd lose your custom product catalog"), offer a discount ("I can get you 20% off this year"), and escalate to the Head of People with FUD about Branded Fit's scale/reliability.

**What they care about:** Retaining the contract. They are not buyers — they are retention agents for the competition.

**How they exert influence:**
- Direct reach to Maya: email/call with a "surprise" counter-offer at renewal.
- Lateral reach to Head of People: escalated call positioning the incumbent as the "safe" enterprise choice.
- FUD tactics: questioning AI-generated mockup quality, Printify's international shipping coverage, or Branded Fit's support SLA.

**How we neutralize them:**
1. **Speed.** Get Maya's live storefront in front of the Head of People before the incumbent knows there is a competitive evaluation. A 10-minute activation turns the demo into a fait accompli.
2. **Switching cost removal.** Branded Fit's onboarding should generate a catalog from scratch — Maya does not need to migrate an existing catalog, so there is nothing to lose.
3. **Proof assets.** Case studies from 3 similar-sized companies (same stage, same swag use case) with redemption lift data inoculate against quality FUD.
4. **Annual contract timing.** If Maya's incumbent contract renews in Q4, begin outreach in Q2–Q3 so the evaluation is complete before the renewal window.

## 3. Organizational Decision Flow

```
TRIGGER: Maya discovers Branded Fit on Slack (#peopleops peer DM or LinkedIn ad)
            |
            ▼
STAGE 1 — Maya's activation (Solo)
  · Pastes domain → storefront preview generated (<10 min)
  · Maya's "wow" moment: sees her company's brand applied to 10 products
  · Maya decides: "I want to try this for real"
  · Duration: 0–3 days
  · Gate: Maya must find the wow. If the AI-generated preview is off-brand, she exits.
            |
            ▼
STAGE 2 — Maya's internal evangelism (Maya → Head of People)
  · Maya shares the preview link + ROI calculator with her Head of People via Slack DM
  · Head of People watches the 2-min Loom or reviews the preview
  · Head of People responds: "Looks good, what's the cost?" → Maya's green light to proceed
  · Duration: 1–5 days
  · Gate: Head of People must not actively object. Common objection: cost. Armed with ROI calc, Maya handles this.
            |
            ▼
STAGE 3 — Brand review (Maya → Head of Brand, if present)
  · Maya shares the "Preview & Edit" link with the Brand Designer
  · Brand Designer adjusts 0–3 elements (a hex code, a product swap)
  · Brand Designer approves → Maya proceeds
  · Duration: 1–3 days (or 0 days if no Brand Designer exists at <150 FTE)
  · Gate: Brand fidelity. No-login edit interface eliminates this gate in most cases.
            |
            ▼
STAGE 4 — Finance / procurement (Maya or Head of People → Controller)
  · Maya submits vendor-onboarding request with Finance FAQ, W-9, MSA, DPA
  · Controller checks budget line, approves PO
  · If IT/InfoSec review required (>200 FTE or security-category company): Trust Center link included
  · Duration: 3–10 days (7–21 days if security review required)
  · Gate: Budget availability and vendor legitimacy. Pre-packaged vendor packet removes friction.
            |
            ▼
STAGE 5 — Purchase (Finance issues PO / Maya enters credit card)
  · $2,400/yr platform fee authorized
  · First swag order scoped (onboarding kit run or all-hands drop)
  · Duration: 1–2 days
            |
            ▼
STAGE 6 — Onboarding to first paid order
  · Maya finalizes product selection, uploads employee CSV, places first order
  · Duration: 3–7 days post-purchase
  · Gate: Printify fulfillment quality on first production run
```

**Median end-to-end (visit to paid):** 14 days (per Step 13)
**P90 (visit to paid):** 28 days (includes security review path)
**Dominant bottleneck:** Stage 4 (Finance/IT) — the single longest gate

## 4. DMU Influence Map

Who can accelerate and who can veto:

| DMU Role | Can Accelerate | Can Veto | Veto Probability | How to Pre-empt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Maya (Champion) | Yes — initiates everything | Yes — if product fails wow moment | 25% (activation failure) | Nail the domain-to-preview flow; brand fidelity is non-negotiable |
| Head of People (Approver) | Yes — budget nod unlocks Finance | Yes — if ROI case doesn't land | 20% (cost objection) | ROI calculator + peer case study from same company stage |
| Controller / Finance (Purchaser) | No | Yes — budget not available | 15% | Pre-packaged vendor packet; target companies where People budget exists |
| Head of Brand (Influencer) | Yes — turns into co-champion | Yes — brand output looks wrong | 30% (when present; absent at <150 FTE) | No-login "Preview & Edit" shareable link |
| IT/InfoSec (Vetter) | No | Yes — security posture insufficient | 40% (when present; rare at <150 FTE) | Trust Center + pre-filled VSQ + no-PII-retention architecture |
| Incumbent AM (Saboteur) | No | Partial — can stall with FUD | 25% (when active contract exists) | Speed + switching-cost removal + redemption-lift proof |

**Net veto-adjusted conversion estimate:**
- At <150 FTE (no Brand/IT roles): ~75% of activated Mayas close
- At 150–300 FTE (Brand + Finance; rare IT): ~55% of activated Mayas close
- At >200 FTE (all roles present): ~35% of activated Mayas close

This is why the tightest beachhead (Step 2's 75–150 FTE band) is operationally superior — fewer veto nodes, faster cycles.

## 6. Sales Motion Implications

### What this DMU map means for Branded Fit's go-to-market

**1. PLG activation is non-negotiable.**
Maya will not advocate internally for a product she has not personally experienced. The product-led hook (domain → storefront in <10 min, no account required for the preview) must work before any sales motion begins. Spending money on outbound to Heads of People before the Maya wow moment is backwards — they have no champion to reinforce their interest.

**2. Maya needs a "sales kit," not a demo.**
Maya does not close deals — she initiates them and then passes off to a 2–4 person internal committee. Branded Fit should automatically generate a Maya-specific "internal sell kit" at the end of her activation: a Loom-style video of her store being generated, a 1-page ROI summary, the Finance FAQ PDF, and a shareable Brand Review link. This transforms Maya from a relay baton into an armed advocate.

**3. Target the 75–150 FTE band first to shorten the sales cycle.**
At <150 FTE, Brand and IT roles are typically absent. The effective DMU is Maya + Head of People + Controller — three people, two conversations, 14-day median cycle. Deliberately targeting this sub-band (already inside Step 2's beachhead) reduces the veto surface by ~40%.

**4. The Finance gate is the primary delay driver — not the Head of People.**
Step 13 estimated a 28-day P90 cycle. Unpacking that: Stage 2 (Head of People) typically takes 1–3 days when Maya is armed with an ROI calculator. Stage 4 (Finance) takes 7–10 days even when the vendor packet is complete — it's calendar-driven (net-30 AP cycles, monthly PO approval batches). Branded Fit should offer month-to-month trial pricing ($299/mo, no annual commitment) as a parallel path that Finance can approve immediately, converting to annual after first order success.

**5. Brand Review link is a product priority, not a nice-to-have.**
At 35% of beachhead accounts (150–300 FTE), the Head of Brand is present and has informal veto power. The no-login "Preview & Edit" shareable link converts this veto node from a blocker into a brand-fidelity proof point. Engineering this before scaling outbound to 150+ FTE companies is critical.

**6. The incumbent AM saboteur is a timing problem.**
60–70% of beachhead accounts already spend $3K–$15K/yr with an incumbent vendor (SwagUp, Stadium, agency). The AM's counter-offer comes at renewal. Branded Fit's outreach should prioritize accounts whose incumbent contract renews in Q3/Q4 — a 30-day outreach window timed 60–90 days before renewal removes the saboteur's leverage. This targeting data (contract renewal date) is often inferable from company job postings mentioning swag vendor transitions or from Apollo.io firmographic signals.

## 8. Open Unknowns

1. **Budget authority thresholds by headcount band.** The $5K–$10K unilateral spend limit assumed for the Head of People is inferred, not surveyed. Five interviews with Heads of People at 75–150 FTE Series A/B companies would confirm or revise this, changing the gating logic between Stage 2 and Stage 4.

2. **Brand Designer presence rate.** The 150-FTE threshold for a Head of Brand/Brand Designer is a LinkedIn-sample estimate. Confirming with a 30-company sample (LinkedIn headcount + creative team size) would allow more precise targeting of "Brand-free" accounts for fastest close.

3. **IT/InfoSec review trigger rate.** What share of 200+ FTE companies in the beachhead actually execute a formal VSQ on a $2,400/yr vendor? The answer may be lower than assumed — many rely on clicking through a standard privacy policy. A 10-prospect survey would calibrate the Stage 4 elongation risk.

4. **Incumbent contract renewal date observability.** The "target 60–90 days before renewal" tactic requires knowing when an account's incumbent contract expires. Apollo.io firmographic signals are limited. A LinkedIn job-posting scan (recent "swag coordinator" or "People Ops" posts mentioning a vendor transition) may be the best proxy — worth testing at 50-account scale.

5. **Head of People influence vs. COO influence at <100 FTE.** At companies below 100 employees, the People function is often the COO or a founding team member. The DMU at these micro-accounts may be a 2-person unit (Maya + COO) with faster cycle times but lower willingness to pay. This sub-segment may warrant a separate, lower-price PLG-only motion.

6. **Actual Stage 4 duration distribution.** The 14-day median and 28-day P90 cycle estimates come from Step 13's reconstruction, not measured cohort data. First 20 paying customers' time-from-activation-to-paid should be logged in the CRM to validate and refine these assumptions.
