# Discovery Call Execution Playbook

## Quick Reference: 30-Minute Call Structure

### **PRE-CALL (5 min before)**
- [ ] Review prospect's company, role, warm intro source
- [ ] Load discovery_calls_tracker.md for note-taking
- [ ] Test audio/video; confirm Zoom/calendar link works
- [ ] Close all notifications and browser tabs except call link and tracker

---

## **CALL STRUCTURE: 30 Minutes**

### **SEGMENT 1: WARMUP (0–2 min)**
**Objective:** Establish rapport, reference warm intro, ask permission

**Your Script:**
> "Hi [Name], thanks so much for jumping on. I appreciate [mutual contact name] connecting us. Quick housekeeping—I want to make sure we have the full 30 minutes, and this is just exploratory. If it's not a fit, I'll tell you straight. Sound good?"

**Listen for:** Tone, enthusiasm level, any immediate objections

---

### **SEGMENT 2: CURRENT STATE (2–8 min)**
**Objective:** Understand their swag workflow, pain points, timeline

**Your Questions (pick 3–4):**
1. "Walk me through how you run a swag drop today. From concept to delivery, what does that look like?"
2. "What's the biggest friction point in that process—is it time, accuracy, or something else?"
3. "How often are you running drops right now?"
4. "What would an ideal drop look like in your head?"

**Golden Rule:** 70% listening, 30% talking. Let them talk for 4+ minutes without interrupting.

**Watch for:** Pain points aligned to Speed / Brand Fidelity / On-Demand Fulfillment

---

### **SEGMENT 3: VALUE PROP TESTING (8–15 min)**
**Objective:** Present mockup/product, gauge credibility, anchor key differentiators

**Your Pitch (3–4 min):**
> "Here's what we do differently: we automate brand extraction using Brandfetch API, which means your mockups come back accurate on first draft. Most vendors hand-design, so you get 5 rounds of revisions. We do 2–3 max, and they're usually just copy tweaks.
>
> Second, we don't lock you into annual contracts. You pay $4.8K for a 50-unit pilot, get 14-day fulfillment guaranteed, and if you love it, we move to $24K annual. If you don't, you're done.
>
> What I want to show you: actual mockup from a similar company, the feedback they gave, and the turnaround timeline."

**Pause. Ask:**
- "Does that resonate with what's broken for you?"
- "What part matters most—the speed or the accuracy?"

**Watch for:** Credibility signals, objections emerging, energy level

---

### **SEGMENT 4: VAN WESTENDORP WTP LADDER (15–23 min)**
**Objective:** Capture WTP at 4 anchors; identify sweet spot

**Read Exactly (no paraphrasing):**
> "I want to test something with you around pricing, and I'm going to read you some price points. For each one, just tell me your gut reaction—is that too cheap to be good, fair price, expensive, or a bargain? There's no right answer; I just want your instinct."

**Anchor 1 — $2,000:**
> "At $2K for a 50-unit pilot, would that be too cheap to be good, fair, expensive, or a bargain?"
- Log their response
- Follow-up: "Why? What would that suggest to you?"

**Anchor 2 — $4,800:**
> "What about $4.8K—same anchors?"
- Log their response
- Follow-up: "At that price, would you seriously consider testing us?"

**Anchor 3 — $24,000:**
> "Now think annual. $24K for unlimited drops per year, managed account, priority support. Too cheap, fair, expensive, or bargain?"
- Log their response
- Follow-up: "If you loved the pilot, would $24K feel like a reasonable annual investment?"

**Anchor 4 — $36,000:**
> "And at $36K—would that be too expensive to even consider?"
- Log their response
- Follow-up: "At what point does it stop making sense for you?"

**Plotting (in your head):**
- If "Fair" or "Bargain" clusters around $4.8K + $24K = strong pricing acceptance
- If "Too Cheap" at $4.8K or "Too Expensive" at $24K = messaging issue or use-case mismatch

---

### **SEGMENT 5: OBJECTIONS & BLOCKERS (23–27 min)**
**Objective:** Surface and categorize top concerns

**Open-Ended Prompt:**
> "Before we wrap, what's your biggest worry about switching vendors or testing something new? What would have to be true for this to feel like a no-brainer?"

**Listen for:** Procurement, vendor lock-in, color accuracy, speed skepticism, multi-brand needs

**If they raise an objection, use this frame:**
> "[Acknowledge objection]. That's actually a really common question. Here's how we handle it: [brief counter]. Does that address it, or is there something deeper?"

**Do NOT oversell.** If they stay skeptical, move to next section.

---

### **SEGMENT 6: PILOT INTENT (27–29 min)**
**Objective:** Gauge Yes/No/Maybe on $4.8K paid pilot; identify activation triggers

**Direct Question:**
> "Based on everything we've talked about, would you be interested in running a $4.8K paid pilot with us in the next 60 days?"

**Responses & Follow-Ups:**

- **"Yes":** 
  - "Awesome. What would you want in the first kit—polos, hoodies, hats?"
  - "When could we kick off?"
  - "Who else needs to sign off—is this your decision, or does [CEO/CFO] need to weigh in?"

- **"Maybe":**
  - "What would move you from maybe to yes?"
  - "Is it price, timing, or do you need to see something else?"
  - "Could we schedule a follow-up for [2 weeks] after you've thought about it?"

- **"No":**
  - "I appreciate the honesty. What's the blocker? Is it not urgent enough, or is there something about our approach that doesn't fit?"
  - "If we weren't in the picture, what vendor would you try?"

**Log the answer + confidence level (1–5)**

---

### **SEGMENT 7: CLOSE (29–30 min)**
**Objective:** Set clear next action; leave door open

**Your Script:**
> "Here's what I'm thinking for next steps: [based on their pilot intent]
>
> If Yes: "I'll send you a 1-page SOW by [date]. You review, sign, and we kick off with brand assets by [date]. Sound good?"
>
> If Maybe: "Let's schedule 20 minutes in 2 weeks. You'll have time to think, and I'll have something new to show you. Does [date] work?"
>
> If No: "No worries. If this changes or you hear about us from someone else, you know where to find me. And if you have someone else you think we'd be good for, I'd love the intro."

**Always end with:**
> "Thanks so much for the time. Really appreciated your straight feedback."

## **POST-CALL (within 1 hour)**

### Immediate Actions:
- [ ] Complete discovery_calls_tracker.md row while memory is fresh
- [ ] Log all Van Westendorp responses + reasoning
- [ ] Copy 2–3 verbatim quotes from speed/fidelity/fulfillment questions
- [ ] Categorize top 3 objections + severity (1–5)
- [ ] Mark pilot intent (Yes/No/Maybe) + confidence

### Within 24 hours:
- [ ] Send follow-up email based on their response:
  - **Yes:** SOW + onboarding checklist
  - **Maybe:** Calendar hold for follow-up + 1 piece of relevant social proof
  - **No:** Warm referral request or offer to stay in touch

## **Common Objections & Quick Counters**

| Objection | Your Counter |
|-----------|--------------|
| **"Color accuracy isn't guaranteed"** | "We use Brandfetch API + human QA. Average accuracy is 4.7/5 from first mockup. If it's off, revisions are free—no rework fees. Have you seen mockups from [recent case study]?" |
| **"We already have a vendor relationship"** | "That's smart. We're not asking you to switch permanently. The $4.8K pilot lets you test us risk-free without breaking your current contract. If you love it, you can transition. If not, you're still with your vendor." |
| **"Need multi-brand support"** | "We support branded drops for now, but we've built the architecture to scale to multi-brand. If you want to pilot that, we can explore it. For a first test, does single-brand work?" |
| **"14 days seems optimistic"** | "I get that skepticism. Here's our guarantee: if we miss 14 days, the kit is free. We have 3 pilots completed with that SLA; all hit on time. Want to see the delivery records?" |
| **"No budget until [next quarter]"** | "Totally understand. Here's what I'd suggest: let's document this now, and we revisit in [timeframe]. I'll send you a pilot proposal so it's on your radar. Deal?" |

## **Dos & Don'ts**

### DO:
✓ Reference their specific pain point from the conversation  
✓ Ask open-ended questions and shut up  
✓ Take notes on everything (you'll forget details)  
✓ Be honest: "I don't know, but I'll find out"  
✓ End on time (respect their calendar)  
✓ Follow up within 24 hours  

### DON'T:
✗ Pitch before understanding their problem  
✗ Oversell or talk over them  
✗ Promise things you can't deliver  
✗ Use jargon ("synergy," "leverage," "best-in-class")  
✗ Skip the Van Westendorp ladder (it's critical data)  
✗ Let "Maybe" drift; schedule the follow-up immediately

By end of discovery call series:

- [ ] Top 3 objections identified (appearing in 3+ calls = pattern)
- [ ] Pilot intent: ≥60% say "Yes" or "Maybe"
- [ ] 2–3 verbatim quotes per prospect per dimension (speed/fidelity/fulfillment)
- [ ] 2–3 pilots signed or in active pipeline
- [ ] ≥80% call completion rate (scheduled calls that actually happen)

## **Troubleshooting**

**Problem:** Prospect is very quiet / not engaging  
**Solution:** Ask more specific questions. "When you say your current vendor is slow, how long does it typically take?" (vs. "What's the timeline?")

**Problem:** Running over 30 min, haven't hit Van Westendorp yet  
**Solution:** Politely interrupt: "This is great, and I don't want to run over. Let me jump to the pricing questions so I respect your time."

**Problem:** Prospect seems interested but won't commit to pilot  
**Solution:** Don't push. Get them to agree to a 2-week follow-up. Often they need to ask CEO/CFO first.

**Problem:** Strong objection you don't have a good counter for  
**Solution:** "That's a really good point. I want to give you a thoughtful answer, not a quick one. Let me loop in my ops team and send you a note by [date]. Would that help?"
